# Chapter 27: A Lot to Unlearn

The next morning, we met at the cafe for breakfast. After we ate, I said, "I don't have much experience with my short sword. Can you help me come up with a training routine? Something that I can run through every morning and evening?"

Vagid put his hand to his chin to consider what I had asked.

Before Vagid could answer, Orrin said, "Sure thing. I know eleven stances. Nine of them are for looking good in doorways."

Nara sighed and shook her head.

"You should ask the Guild. They have several instructors on staff," said Nara.

"I agree with Nara. That's your best way to get better with the sword," said Vagid.

"Hm. Okay, I'll ask Prue about it," I said.

"Prue?" said Vagid. "Another dinner?"

"Well, it's not like there is a limit on the number of times I can take her out to dinner," I said.

"Was it easier to ask her the second time?" asked Nara. She knew that was what Vagid really wanted to ask.

"Much easier," I said. "I wish I had asked her sooner."

I glanced at Vagid. He suddenly found his empty breakfast plate very interesting.

After breakfast, we each went our separate ways. I walked to the Guild and got into Prue's line. Once I got to the front, she said, "You're a bit early for dinner."

"Never keep a pretty lady waiting," I said.

Prue's expression didn't change, but she still blushed a little bit.

"I really came in to ask about training. I'm not great with my sword, and Nara said the Guild has instructors," I said. "Do I need to sign up or something?"

"Aren't you a bit young to be having trouble with your sword?" asked Prue.

I felt my face and ears grow hot.

"The one on my belt," I said, still blushing. "The metal one."

"Stella will want to appraise your skill level. Let me check with her. She may be able to test you today," said Prue as she stood up and walked over to a woman who looked about two hundred years old. Stooped over with a cane.

*This must be the instructor's grandmother.*

Prue chatted with the old woman for a second, then came back. "Stella said that she could check you out right now," said Prue. "You're really lucky."

"Great. Thank you," I said. "Was that the instructor's grandmother?"

"Something like that," said Prue. "Just head back to the training grounds behind the Guild. Stella will be waiting."

I went straight to the training grounds and looked around. I didn't see anyone. Suddenly, I felt a searing pain in the back of my leg and fell to the ground.

"Aaah!" I yelped.

"Well, you're not very observant," said the little old lady I had seen earlier. "I'm Stella. And so far I'm not impressed."

I got up slowly, still feeling the pain in my leg. Stella looked me up and down, and then shook her head and said, "It looks like I'll need to break all your bad habits first."

"Don't you want me to at least draw my sword?" I asked.

"Why bother? You don't know how to use it," Stella said. "You don't even know how to move the right way."

*What does that mean?*

"What did you hit me with?" I asked. "It felt like you stabbed me."

"Hah! I poked you... with my finger," said Stella. She held up her hand with her pointer finger extended, then poked me in the shoulder. It felt like she ran me through with a spear. The pain hit so hard that I couldn't even make a noise. I just stood there with my mouth open and my eyes bulging.

Stella slapped my back and suddenly I could move again.

"What the hell was that?" I asked.

"Time for another demonstration?" asked Stella.

"No! I was just curious," I said. "Can I train with you?"

"Before I agree to let you train, we need to have a quick fight, so that I can see what you can do," said Stella.

"Okay," I said and stepped back into a fighting stance I had seen in a movie, fists pointing forward.

"Hah! You're going to need your sword. That's what you wanted to learn, right?" said Stella.

"Um, won't you get hurt if I use my sword?" I asked.

"Not even if you had half the Guild helping you," said Stella.

*Who is this woman?*

I drew my sword and settled into the stance that Orrin had shown me.&#32;

"Well, are you going to hit me or just look silly?" asked Stella.

I took a step forward and swung like I was swatting a fly with a newspaper. Stella moved as soon as I did, but toward me instead of away. She stepped inside my swing and turned with it. The next thing I knew, I was staring up at the clear sky. *How did I miss her?* I didn't pass out or anything, but whatever Stella did was too fast for me to see.

Stella had me repeat this "test" until midafternoon. By the third time, I had stopped trying to hit her and started trying to land somewhere soft. I was sweating and my legs and arms were shaking from fatigue. Finally, she said, "You have a lot to learn and a lot to unlearn. The beginners' class starts at sunrise."

*Wait, what?*

"So, you'll train me?" I asked, wheezing out the words.

"Yeah. I've known that since I poked you," said Stella.

"Then what was all the rest of this for?" I asked.

"I wanted to see what kind of stamina you had," she said. "You've got none."

I nodded and thanked Stella for letting me join the training. I left and went to the chapel to get cleaned up before dinner with Prue.&#32;

I walked into the chapel, well mainly limped, and saw Halden. He looked at me limping in and said, "Rocks?"

"Stella," I said.

"Hmf," said Halden. "Want me to heal your leg? I can do it for just three sparks. I can add it to your tab."

"Nah," I said. "I can use it to get sympathy when I meet Prue for dinner later."

"That works. I've been pretty busy lately, healing people from Thornspire. Funny thing, none of them will say what happened," said Halden. Then he looked at me. "Probably rocks. I can always make time for my best customer."

I quickly wiped myself down to get rid of the smell of sweat and changed into clean clothes. When I finished, it was late afternoon, so I left for the Guild.

At the Guild, I went inside and waited for Prue to finish for the day.

When Prue was done, she came over to where I was standing and said, "Did you and Stella have fun?"

"That's one way to put it," I said. "I'll be limping for a while. You said I was lucky."

"You are. Stella usually breaks something," said Prue.

*Agree to disagree.*

"Where would you like to try for dinner?" I asked.

"There's a new restaurant near the curry shop," said Prue. "I thought we could give it a try."

I reached out my hand, which Prue took. After a few steps of me limping, Prue decided that holding on to my arm would make me more stable. We walked that way to the new restaurant. I decided Stella could poke me again tomorrow.

We got to the restaurant and were seated at a table, and I stared at the menu. I couldn't figure out what anything on it meant.

I sighed and said under my breath, "I'd kill for a pizza."

"A what?" asked Prue.

"Pizza. It's a flat, round bread, with tomato sauce, melted cheese and toppings on top."

"So, it's bread with cheese on it."

"... When you say it like that, yes."

"And where did you run into this cheesy bread?" asked Prue.

"It's something that I used to eat where I grew up," I said.

"Oh, where is that?" asked Prue.

*Crap.*

"It's far from here. How was your day?" I asked.

"That was a very obvious change of subject," said Prue. "My day was fine."

We discussed the menu options, then I ordered the noodles with orc sausage and Prue ordered the seared orc steak.

We waited for our food in silence. I read the menu again, in case it had learned English. When the plates arrived, we started to eat.

*The sausage is good. I don't want to know what part of the orc.*

"You do that a lot," said Prue.

"Do what?" I asked.

"Look at things like you've never seen them before. The menu. The cutlery. The orc."

"It's a big world," I said.

"It's a small town," said Prue, and went back to her steak.

"Two soldiers came by this afternoon," said Prue. "They wanted to see the list of adventurers who've registered in the last few months."

My fork stopped halfway to my mouth.

"What did you tell them?" I asked.

"Nothing. I'm a desk clerk. Mara told them the registry was being reorganized and to come back in a week." Prue took a bite. "Mara has never reorganized anything in her life."

*A week. We only have a week.*

"Why do you look like that?" asked Prue.

"Stella. I'm thinking about sunrise tomorrow," I said a bit lamely.

"You still have all your bones. That's a good first day," answered Prue.

"Yeah," I said.

Prue looked at me for a moment before returning to her dinner.

We finished dinner. I got up and held out my hand to Prue. She looked at me for a second, then took my arm. We walked like that back to her house.

Before letting go of my arm, she gave it a brief squeeze and said, "Get some rest. You look like you've got a lot on your mind."

"Yeah," I said. "Thank you, Prue. Good night."

I walked back to the river where we had carved Plan B's message on the boulder and sat on the bench.

*Tired Guy, something came up,* I said.

*Hello, Evan. How was your dinner?* asked Tired Guy.

*There are guards asking about recently enrolled adventurers at the Guild,* I said.

*I believe that was inevitable, *said Tired Guy.&#32;

*Inevitable? That's it? That's your whole reaction? They are going to find me and everyone here is in danger! *I said.

*It's my fault! *I said. *I put Prue and all of my friends in danger! I can't stay here! I have to leave so they'll** be safe!*

*Think about what happens after you leave,* said Tired Guy. *The soldiers come back in a week for a list of names. One of the names has vanished. They'll ask who he spoke to and who he ate dinner with. You wouldn't be moving the danger away. You'd be pointing it at her*.

*What can I do?* I pleaded. *I want to protect Prue and everyone, but everything I do seems to be wrong.*

*You keep deciding for other people,* said Tired Guy.

*I'm not deciding for them. I'm protecting them,* I said. *...Am I?*

*I would have to tell Prue about what I am, *I said. *She might not want to be with me if she knows**.*

*And she might still want to be with you,* said Tired Guy.

I opened my mouth to argue, and couldn't find the argument. If putting distance between us wouldn't make her safer, maybe the answer was to stop deciding for her and ask.

*Thank you. Good night,* I said.

*Good night, Evan,* said Tired Guy.

*Soldiers, a deadline, and a girl I couldn't lie to. And in a few hours, a tiny old woman is going to poke me with one finger.*
