# Chapter 23: I Love Dinner

Before starting our watches, Plan B talked it over and unanimously decided to pay Tobin for letting us stay in his workshop. His family had lost enough and needed the money more than we did. Mave had charged three rays and two sparks for the four of us, and that included dinner. We decided on two rays each, which was a lot more than Mave would have charged. That was the point. We left the money in a small pouch on the workshop table with a note.

> This is payment for letting us stay in the workshop. Thank you. Plan B.

In the morning, we joined Tobin and his family to lay Mardin and Sava to rest. It was a simple ceremony. We dug a small hole, placed the skulls, the jacket, and the shoe inside, then covered them with dirt. Tobin's wife had brought two small saplings, which she planted side by side in the soil over the grave.

Afterward, we walked Tobin and his family back to their house.

"Tobin, thank you for having us," said Vagid. "We need to get back to the Guild and make our report, but we'll try to stop in again if we're in the area."

"Thank you for taking care of the kobolds. I know the pay isn't much..." said Tobin.

"The pay is more than enough," said Orrin.

"Yes. We enjoyed visiting with your family," said Nara.

"Just take care of them. And if something like this happens again, let the Guild know and ask for Plan B," I said.

"I will," said Tobin.

Right then, Ronar came running up. "Bye-bye, pretty lady!" he said to Nara. Nara blushed a little, then scooped him up for a quick hug before setting him back down. Ronar ran off to play.

We walked back around the wall to Lumenhold's gate side and found an inn where we could arrange the coach trip back to Millbrook. The coach left at noon and stopped at dusk, at a different inn from the one on our way out. We left first thing the next morning and arrived in Millbrook just before dinner.

We collected our gear from the coach and headed to the Guild to report the job. At the Guild, Vagid, Nara, and Orrin said they would wait by the door while I took care of the report. I suspected they just wanted to see me squirm.

I joined Prue's line. After a few minutes, I reached the front. Prue was leaning forward on an elbow with her chin in her hand.

"Hi, Prue," I said. "We completed the Lower Lumenhold job." I put the bag of kobold ears on the counter.

Prue looked at me with a blank expression, then sighed. "I really hate my job," she said, picking up the bag. It didn't take her long to count the ears.

"Twenty-three? Hmm," said Prue, and she left for the cashier to get our pay. Instead of four pouches, she came back and handed me four sparks. "I trust you can divide up the pay yourself," she said.

"I can probably manage," I said. "But if you're free tomorrow after work, would you go to dinner with me?"

Prue's expression changed from blank to surprised. "I think I can manage that," she said, with a small smile.

"I'll meet you here tomorrow then," I said.

Prue sighed. "Yeah, sure," she said, then wrote something on a blank job slip and handed it to me.

On my way back to the rest of Plan B, I read the slip.

> I'm looking forward to dinner. —P

I smiled, refolded the slip, and put it in my pocket.

"My, don't you look like the cat who got the bird," said Nara. "So, are you taking Prue to dinner tonight?"

"No. Tomorrow night," I said, nervously.

Vagid looked a bit surprised. "And you just asked her? To dinner? Just like that?"

"Yeah. Just like that," I said.

"That's usually how it works," said Orrin. "What did you think he would do, send her a note?"

Vagid's face and ears turned bright red, but his expression stayed blank. Then he held out his hand. I put a single spark in his palm, and he turned toward the Guild door and started walking.

"Let's meet for breakfast tomorrow," I said, just as he was walking out the door.

Orrin started laughing, and Nara just smiled and shook her head.

"Really, Orrin, why do you make fun of him?" asked Nara.

"It's fun," said Orrin. "Plus, maybe it'll push him to actually take a risk."

I said goodbye and headed back to the chapel. When I got there, I found Halden sitting on the stone bench beside the chapel door.

"No healing?" asked Halden.

"Nope. I didn't get hurt this time," I said.

"Hmf," said Halden. Then he went into the chapel.

I left my gear in my room, then headed for the river. I walked to the spot where I could read the partial message Nara had carved into the boulder.

> IF YOU'RE FROM EARTH, YOU'RE NOT ALONE.

*We need to finish the message tomorrow.*

I sat on the stone bench.

*Tired Guy?* I asked.

*Hello, Evan,* said Tired Guy. *How did your Guild job go?*

*We killed the kobolds. The little girl's father was there too. The kobolds had killed and eaten them both,* I said. *We brought them back to Lower Lumenhold and laid them to rest there.*

*I'm sure they would have appreciated that,* said Tired Guy.

*The kobolds' cave opens into the Lumenhold dungeon, so we'll be going back,* I said.

*That sounds promising, and dangerous,* said Tired Guy. *Please be careful.*

*I also asked Prue out to dinner,* I said. *I'm really nervous about it.*

*This, too, is part of life,* said Tired Guy. *I hope your dinner goes well.*

*Thanks*, I said. *Is it weird that I'm more nervous about dinner with Prue than I am about breaking into the Lumenhold archives?*

*Actually, that is quite normal,* said Tired Guy, and it sounded like he was smiling. *Get some rest.*

I returned to the chapel and went to bed. Tomorrow was going to be a busy day.

In the morning, I found Nara at the cafe when I got there.

"Good morning, Nara," I said.

"Good morning, Evan," said Nara, before taking a sip of her tea.

I ordered eggs and sausage along with tea. While waiting, I asked, "Do you think we have time to finish the boulder today?"

"Yes," said Nara. "We really need to get that done."

Orrin and Vagid arrived at the same time, from the same direction. *That's odd.*

"Don't you two live on opposite sides of the cafe?" I asked. "You both just came from the center of town."

"I came from home," said Orrin, smirking.

"I... uh... came from the... Guild," said Vagid. "I had some business there."

Nara sipped her tea, then said, "At dawn?"

"The Guild doesn't open until after breakfast," said Orrin, still smirking.

Vagid's ears and face turned beet red.

I changed the subject. "Can we finish the boulder by the river after breakfast?"

"Yes!" said Vagid quickly.

We finished our meal without teasing Vagid anymore, then headed to the boulder to finish our message.

This time, Vagid, Orrin, and I each borrowed two buckets to carry the sand. Halden was happy to lend them to us as long as we returned them in the same condition.

When we got there, Nara sat down on the stone bench and started on the final line while the rest of us hauled sand. Orrin took his buckets upstream, and Vagid and I went back to our old sandbar downstream. By our second trip, the sandbar was mostly underwater. By our third, Vagid's arms were shaking so badly that he spilled half a bucket on his boots. When we got back, Orrin was already stretched out on the bench.

"Taking a break?" I asked.

"Nara said that's enough sand," said Orrin, without opening his eyes.

We washed out the buckets and set them on the bank to dry.

Nara kept carving the boulder using the sand from the pile. An hour later, she came over and sat on the bench with us.

"I think it's done," said Nara. "At least it looks like the same squiggles."

I looked at the boulder and read the finished message:

> IF YOU'RE FROM EARTH, YOU'RE NOT ALONE.&#32;
>
> ASK THE GUILD FOR PLAN B'S FILE.

"It's perfect, Nara," I said. "You did an amazing job!"

"I'm just glad that it's done," said Nara.

"On a different topic," I said hesitantly. "Can I ask you something?"

Nara looked at me, then smiled. "About your dinner?"

"Yeah," I said. "I'm not too sure how dates work here. Do I need to bring anything when I pick her up? Are there any expectations for me on the date?"

"Hah!" said Orrin. "Pick her up, take her to dinner, and don't forget to get a few kisses when you drop her off."

Nara rolled her eyes. "Evan, dates here are simple. You meet somewhere, then go to dinner and just talk to each other. You're not trying to propose. You're just trying to get to know each other over a meal."

"So, I don't need to bring flowers or candy or anything?" I asked.

"What's 'can-dee'?" asked Orrin.

"You don't need to bring anything," said Nara. "Just yourself and some good conversation."

Vagid was silent through the entire conversation, but I could see him making mental notes. I often forgot they were several years younger than me. I had assumed they had all dated before. Not each other, but other people. Maybe I wasn't the only one who was new to dating in this world.

With the carving complete, we took the buckets back to the chapel and put them where Halden wanted them.

We decided to break for lunch and walked back to the cafe. We each ordered, then I asked Nara if she would go with me to look around the market after lunch.

"I can, but what are you looking for?" asked Nara.

"I don't know, but I thought it might be nice to give Prue a small token at the end of the night, something to remember our dinner by," I said. "Something small, like a pin or hair clip."

Nara thought about it, then smiled. "Okay."

I turned to Orrin and Vagid. "You're welcome to come too, if you don't have anything better to do."

*Maybe all of us guys can learn something about dating.*

After we finished lunch, Nara led Vagid, Orrin, and me to the market. She brought us to a little stall that sold earrings, bracelets, and other trinkets.

"Hello again, Nara," said the shopkeeper. "Are you looking for anything in particular today?"

"Not for me today. My friend is looking for something," said Nara, nodding at me.

"Oh? Anything in particular? For anyone in particular?" asked the shopkeeper.

"Um... it's just a token I wanted to give a girl. For our first dinner," I said.

The shopkeeper smiled kindly, then pointed to some hair clips. "These are nice, and they're not expensive."

One of them was a blue-gray carving of a bird in flight. As soon as I saw it, I thought about how nice Prue would look wearing it. I pointed to it. "That one. Can I get it, please?"

"Of course," said the shopkeeper, picking up the hair clip. "That will be one ray, please."

I paid the ray and carefully put the hair clip in my pocket. Then I turned to Nara. "That was easier than I thought."

"It was a nice choice," said Nara.

"But why that specific hair clip?" asked Vagid.

"The color matches Prue's eyes," I said.

Nara smiled at that. Vagid looked like he was making more mental notes. Orrin seemed to be ignoring us completely.

We said our goodbyes and agreed to meet for breakfast in the morning.

I headed back to the chapel and asked Halden about getting cleaned up.

"Hmf. A bath is a lot of work, and it takes a while to heat enough water. There's a basin in your room if you just want to wash up with a cloth," said Halden.

I went back to my room and scrubbed down with the basin and a cloth. It wasn't a hot shower, but it beat showing up for a date smelling like sweat and river sand. Then I changed into clean clothes.

It was getting close to dinnertime, so I told Halden I was going to dinner and left.

"Hmf" was the only response I got.

The walk to the Guild was leisurely, and it was almost closing time when I got there. I went inside and stood by the door. I didn't want to interfere with her work.

A little while later, they began closing up the counter. Cuthbert came down the stairs and said good night to the receptionists before he left. Mara waved to me on her way out.

Then Prue came over.

"Ready?" asked Prue.

"Yes. You?" I asked.

"Yes," said Prue, and she walked to the door. I hurried over and held it open for her. As she walked past me, I asked, "Is there a place you'd like to try for dinner?"

"Yes. There's a place off the town center that serves a spicy dish called curry," said Prue. "Della introduced Mara and me to it."

*Curry? Here?*

"Perfect. Let's go," I said, and held out my hand to her.

Prue looked at me for a second, then took my hand. She led me to the curry house just off the town center, and we got a table. Prue ordered a medium curry.

"I'll have the same," I said.

"Prue," I asked. "Why would you want to go to dinner with me? I'm not special or anything."

Prue chuckled. "You joined one of the better parties in the Guild and became a valued member quickly. You don't seem to have any magic, and you aren't that good at combat."

*Well, you're not wrong.*

"But you're always kind to everyone you meet. You listen to people. You care about others. To me, that's pretty special," said Prue, smiling.

I could feel my ears and face heating up.

"Now, why would someone special like you want to go to dinner with me?" asked Prue.

"You're gruff. You don't like your job. You're not perky," I said.

"But you work hard, and you're very efficient. You care about people, but you hide it behind dramatic sighs and snarky comments. And you're really pretty," I said. "Basically, you're very special too."

Now it was Prue's turn to blush. We saw each other's red faces and laughed.

Our food arrived, and we ate, joked, and talked about anything we could think of. Finally, the owner came out and asked us to leave so he could close up. I hadn't noticed the time, but it was quite late. I paid for dinner, then held out my hand to Prue. She took it, and I asked, "Where should I drop you off?"

"My place is on a street behind the Guild, but I can make it by myself," said Prue.

"Nara would turn me into a toad if I didn't walk you to your house and make sure you didn't run into trouble," I said.

"Nara is overprotective sometimes," said Prue.

"Well, I'm not going to risk it," I said, and started walking toward the Guild.

We reached the house Prue pointed out, and I stopped a few steps from her door.

"Prue, please take this," I said, and handed her the hair clip. "I saw it at the market, and it reminded me of you."

"You see me as a bird?" asked Prue.

"A caged bird who wants to be free and soar in the sky," I said.

It was hard to see in the dark, but I thought Prue smiled at that.

"Thank you for a great dinner," Prue said, then lightly kissed my cheek. "Good night, Evan."

"Good night, Prue."

Prue went inside. I stood there a moment and touched my cheek where she had kissed me. This had been a really good day.

I started back to the chapel.

*Tired Guy?* I thought.

*Good evening, Evan. How did your dinner go?* asked Tired Guy.

*It went well. I had fun,* I said.

*That is all we can ever hope for in life,* said Tired Guy. *Get some rest and have happy dreams.*

*Good night,* I said, and continued my walk back to the chapel.

I slept soundly that night. No happy dreams, but no unhappy ones either.

In the morning, I met Plan B at the cafe as planned. Orrin, Vagid, and Nara were already there when I arrived.

"So, how many kisses?" asked Orrin. "Did you stay the ni—"

Nara elbowed Orrin, then asked me, "How was dinner?"

"It was great," I said. "We had a good time. We ate at a place just off the town center. We both had curry."

"I've always wanted to try that place," said Vagid.

"The curry is delicious," said Nara. "Maybe you should take someone to dinner there too."

Vagid suddenly became very interested in his breakfast.

"Is curry a local dish?" I asked.

"It's always been around, as far as I know. If you want to know its history, I suppose you could ask the cook," said Nara. "Why?"

"There's a dish called curry back home too. It tastes similar, so I was curious where it came from," I said.

We ate in silence for a bit. Then Vagid said, "When we're done here, we should go to the river landing."

We all nodded. Once we finished, we walked to the landing, where no one could overhear us.

"So, what are our next steps?" I asked.

"We have a way into the castle now, but we don't know where the archives are. If you can check with Tired Guy, maybe he has an idea, or some memories of where they are," said Vagid. "I'll see if I can get any information from my brother. I just don't want to drag him into this unless we have to."

"I can see about getting a date with one of the maids to find out if they know anything," said Orrin, grinning.

"Isn't that a long way to go for a date?" asked Nara. "Your dates can wait until we get there."

"If we can get any information today, we can discuss it on the trip there tomorrow," said Vagid. "Otherwise, we'll spend a lot of time searching around and hoping not to get caught."

Vagid left to check with his brother, and I headed to the river. When I got to the boulder, I sat on the stone bench and cleared my mind.

*Tired Guy, I need some information,* I said.

*If it is within my power to give, then it is yours, Evan,* said Tired Guy.

*Do Rachel's memories have anything about the archives in Lumenhold?* I asked. *Specifically, where they are.*

After a few minutes, Tired Guy said, *Rachel remembers a bookcase beside a large statue of a minotaur. A switch on the bookcase swings it open, and the archives are behind it. The room with the statue is beneath a tower. A stairway at the tower's base leads down to it.*

*Thank you,* I said. *I know going through her memories is painful for you.*

*I need to rest, Evan. Be careful,* said Tired Guy.

I caught up with Nara and Orrin, who were buying supplies: rope, covered lanterns, and food. Vagid joined us while we were gathering the last of it.

"Any luck?" I asked Vagid.

"My brother was on his way to work when I ran into him. He didn't want to stand around and talk," said Vagid. "How about you?"

"Some. Let's talk about it at the landing," I said.

We carried our supplies down to the landing.

"Tired Guy found something in Rachel's memories. The Chancellor has a secret archive below the base of a tower, in a room with a minotaur statue. The bookcase there has a switch that opens it," I said.

"It's a large castle, but it only has so many towers. It wouldn't be in a tower on the outer wall, because that could be destroyed if the castle were attacked. That leaves two, maybe three towers. And they should be easy to spot," said Vagid. "The one we want will probably have guards."

"If it has guards, how will we get past them?" asked Orrin.

"We'll scout the castle and make a plan once we know which tower it is," said Vagid.

The coaches to Lumenhold always left at noon, and it was already past noon, so we would have to wait until tomorrow. That worked out, since I needed to let Prue know I would be away for a few days. We agreed to meet at the cafe in the morning. After we said our goodbyes, I walked to the Guild, joined Prue's line, and waited my turn.

When I got to the front of the line, Prue gave me a small smile. "You again?"

"I'm like a bad cold. I just won't go away," I said, smiling. "I'm going out of town with Plan B. We need to do some investigating. I just wanted to let you know."

"Investigating? You sound mysterious," said Prue. "Well, be careful."

Then, on a whim, I asked, "Do you know if Mara likes Vagid?"

Prue turned toward Mara. "Mara, do you like Vagid?"

"Vagid? He's nice," said Mara.

"Would you go out to dinner with him if he asked?" I asked Mara.

"Sure! I love dinner!" said Mara.

I stared at Mara for a second, then turned to Prue, who just shrugged.

I waved to Prue as I left. She just nodded and went back to sighing dramatically.

The next morning, I gathered my gear for the trip and walked to the cafe. Vagid was already there, but Nara and Orrin hadn't arrived yet.

"Morning," I said.

Vagid nodded and kept eating his breakfast.

"Did you tell Prue that you'd be away for a while?" asked Vagid.

"Yep," I said. "I still can't tell if she considers me relationship material or not."

"I've never been able to figure that out," said Vagid.

I decided not to tell him about "I love dinner."

Orrin arrived next, ordered eggs with double sausage, and immediately started stuffing his face. *Not even a hello.*

Nara arrived as Orrin was eating his last sausage.

"Good morning, everyone," said Nara. "Chew with your mouth closed, please, Orrin."

"Mrumf," said Orrin with a mouth full of sausage.

We finished our meal, then went to arrange the coach to Lumenhold.

The last time I'd gone to Lumenhold, I'd come back with twenty-three kobold ears and left two saplings growing over a grave. I wondered what I'd bring back this time.
