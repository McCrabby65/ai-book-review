# Chapter 39: Still Looking

I continued along the causeway, avoiding the areas that were clearly about to collapse. Eventually, I came to a gap in the causeway. Nothing but water filling the space. With the light fading, it was impossible to tell what was under the water. It could be a few feet or bottomless.

*Tired Guy? I don't suppose you can help me walk on water?*

Nothing.

*I did spend most of Patton's Run underwater. How bad could it be?*

*Well, Twelve is a water mage, so getting in the water here is probably a really bad idea.*

Vagid, Orrin and Nara came up behind me.

I was still trying to figure out a way across when the water started rising. Filling the gap.

"Did he send you to finish it?" said a voice from above. It had come from the window with the light.

I saw Orrin reach for an arrow, careful not to make any noise.

The water in the lake shot up like a giant tentacle and punched a hole in the hillside across the shore. We all froze. Orrin put his hand back down.

I slowly raised my empty hands and held them above my head.

"Nobody sent us. I'm the one they summoned to replace you." Then, in English: "Thirteen."

I paused. Then I switched back to Hallish. "It didn't work. They called me defective and threw me in the river." *After some gratuitous torture.*

There was a pause. "You can come up. Tell the rest to stay put."

I looked at each of my friends and nodded. "Where can I get across the gap?"

"Just walk across," he said.

I put a foot out onto the water in the gap and put some weight on it. Amazingly, it held. I put my other foot on the water. *Okay.*

I walked across the gap and along the causeway. I heard footsteps behind me and guessed it was Nara. Vagid wouldn't try it. I would never hear Orrin, so that left Nara.

I walked through the doorway at the base of the keep. There was a staircase that spiraled up the tower. I started up the stairs and heard an echoing set of footsteps behind me.

As I neared the top, I could see the entrance to the room with the light. I reached the landing and stopped at the doorway. "Hello?" I called.

From where I stood just outside the doorway, I couldn't see anyone, and no one answered. I took a step into the room and waited. "Hello?" I tried again.

The room had shelves from floor to ceiling. They were filled with models of buildings from Earth, every one of them extremely detailed. I walked over to one beside the doorway. It was a cathedral with one finished steeple and one with scaffolding around it. I had seen this before, but where?

I reached out to touch the model when I heard, "Don't."

I froze. I slowly put my hand back down and turned around. There, sitting in a chair, was a man in his late fifties or early sixties. He looked like he was just waiting to die.

For some reason, I knew his face. I could swear that we had never met, but I had seen him somewhere before.

"Hi. My name is Evan McIntyre," I said.

"It doesn't matter," said the man. *Okay.*

"I was summoned a little over two and a half months ago."

"If you came to kill me, you could have saved yourself the walk," he said.

"I came to talk to you."

He leaned forward and stared at me, like he was looking at a lab specimen. "You really don't have any magic."

"None," I agreed. "I arrived like this. It was not what they were expecting."

"I'm sure." He chuckled. "It must have been quite the surprise. My name is Johan Nilsson."

"Johan, can I ask what the date was when you left Earth?"

He told me. It was just over five weeks before my summon date.

"I know this will sound ridiculous. But there is a time difference between Earth and this world. I was able to find notes from previous summons and verified it. Do you know about it?"

"Time difference? What time difference?" asked Johan.

"From the information that I have, one year here is only one day on Earth."

For the first time, his expression changed from despair to shock. "That can't be!" he said.

I told him what the date was when I was summoned.

He started to cry, not wracking sobs, just tears slowly flowing down his face. "All for nothing."

"Sorry? I'm not sure what you mean."

"They promised that if I only did the tasks they wanted, that they would send me back. To the same time that I left. It would be as if no time had passed," he said.

"Time doesn't work that way," I said. *At least I don't think it does.*

He looked old and frail, but that didn't make him harmless. I wanted to connect with him somehow. I just needed to find how to do that. I looked around the room and came back to the model of the cathedral I was looking at when I first came in.

"These models are amazing. This cathedral looks familiar to me for some reason. Can you tell me about it?"

"Sankta Ingrids domkyrka i Granholm," said Johan. "It means Saint Ingrid's Cathedral in Granholm."

"Did you live near it?" I asked.

"No. I lived close enough so that I could visit it on the weekends," he said.

*That's it!*

"A couple of months ago, I went on a trip with my ex-girlfriend. At one of our stops, we pulled into a grocery store. I remember seeing a postcard with pictures of this cathedral there."

Suddenly, I was grabbed and lifted up by my collar. Johan was very spry for an old guy.

"WHAT?" he yelled. Then he slowly set me down again. I was still shocked that he was so strong and quick. I had zero chance against him—that was now clear.

"What was the name of the town you stopped in? Do you remember?" he asked.

"I don't remember," I said.

"Was it Lindmora?" asked Johan.

"It might have been. It's been a couple of months and we saw a lot of places," I said.

"You said you stopped at a grocery store. Do you remember the name of the store?" he asked.

"I'm sorry. I don't remember," I said.

"Can you remember anything about the store? The exterior? Any signs they had up?" he asked.

"Not much. There was a logo on the store sign. It had a tree leaf of some kind," I said.

Something in Johan's face eased. He actually smiled. Just a small one.

"Lindmoras Livs," said Johan. He pointed at a model of a grocery store on the windowsill. I walked over to take a better look. When I got closer, I could see the little leaf on its sign. It was the same one I was trying to describe.

"I remember seeing a missing-person poster," I said. I wracked my brain to remember the poster. It was of a guy about my age. He had a mole on his left cheek and I guessed black hair—it was hard to tell on the poster since it was in black and white.

I looked at Johan and noticed that he had a mole on his left cheek. *Crap.*

"You," I said quietly. "It was you on the poster. They were looking for you. They're still looking for you."

Johan stared at me. "I thought they didn't care," he said. "That I didn't matter."

"You matter to someone. They put up a poster."

He was staring at the floor, but he nodded.

Johan sighed. "But this doesn't matter. Neither of us has any way to get back."

"Not yet," I said.

Johan looked at me. "What do you mean?"

"I mean I haven't found a way. That doesn't mean there isn't one. I'm not done looking."

I was facing Johan with the doorway behind him. I heard a quiet creak, and looked up to see Nara slowly take her hand off her knife.

Johan was quiet for a long time. Then, very softly: "Is it still up? The poster?"
