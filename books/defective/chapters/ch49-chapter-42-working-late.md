# Chapter 42: Working Late

I led Johan through town to the curry house. All the tables inside were occupied, so we had to sit at a table on their open-air porch. I picked a seat that faced the street, and Johan sat opposite me.

"This smell... is it curry?" asked Johan.

"Yep," I said. "Order whatever you want. My treat."

I ordered a medium curry, and Johan ordered a hot curry. *He's braver than I am.*

"Do you have any questions for me?" I asked Johan in English. "Ones that you might not have wanted the others to hear?"

"You never answered if you were going to go back," said Johan, looking at the table.

"I honestly don't know. I have family there still, but we don't talk anymore," I said. "Then there's someone here that I like a lot."

"You've gotten close to someone?" asked Johan.

"Yeah. She works at the Guild," I said. "We're still working things out, but I think it's going good."

"That's good," said Johan. "I never got the chance to get close to anyone. They kept me isolated."

"What they did to us was wrong," I said. "But we have a chance to make sure no one else has to go through that."

"Will it be enough?" asked Johan.

"Enough?" I asked.

"To make up for everything that I've done. All the death," said Johan.

"I don't know," I said, "but it's a start. Every journey begins with the first step." I winced. I'd just quoted a motivational poster at a man who'd drowned a town.

A server brought our curry. "Which got the hot curry?"

I pointed to Johan. They set the plate down, then put the other plate in front of me. This smelled delicious. *If only we had naan too.*

This entire time, Johan's expression had been that of a man resigned to his fate. He took a bite of the curry, and for the first time his expression changed. Shock, then slowly a smile. *Yep.*

After he swallowed, he laughed and said, "It tastes just like my curry."

Then I explained the history of the curry house and the cook's grandmother.

"Ah. That explains it," said Johan. "The carrier who brought the spices asked me why I wanted the spices, so I made him some curry. He took notes."

Then Johan paused. "That was the last time I ordered spices. After that, it just didn't seem important anymore. Nothing did."

Johan took another bite, and I started in on my curry.

Down the street, I saw Prue and Mara walking toward the curry house. Mara was laughing at something. Then she stopped dead, grabbed Prue's arm, and pulled her into an alley.

*That's weird.*

I had just put a spoonful in my mouth when a woman pulled a chair up to our table and sat down. *That's weirder.* I looked at her, then at Johan.

Johan shrugged.

"Can I help you?" I asked.

She was wearing an all-black outfit. Her hair was dark and short, like a buzz cut. She had a thin black veil pushed back. Her face was pretty, and definitely not one I'd seen before.

The woman didn't say anything.

"You have the wrong table," I said, and started to raise my hand for a server.

"Put your hand down, or I will have to involve others," said the woman quietly.

"Others? Involved? How?" I asked.

"You"—she indicated Johan—"are Twelve."

She looked at me. "And you are Thirteen."

"I think your'e confused," I said.

"You were just speaking Otherborn," she said. "I may not know what you said, but I know what it sounds like."

"I was sent to eliminate Twelve, but now that I have both, I will take you to Lumenhold," she said.

*Mara saw her. Mara hid. Whoever this woman was, a retired assassin hadn't wanted to be seen by her.*

I wasn't sure what to say to her, but I wasn't looking forward to leaving with her.

"Why would we go anywhere with you?" I asked.

"Because otherwise, I will be forced to make a scene," she said, "and the carnage will be much worse."

I looked at Johan. He was sitting with his hands in his lap, staring at the table.

"You will leave money on the table for the meal. Then you will both get up and leave. You will walk east to the first alleyway on the right. Then you will enter it," she said.

I looked around at the people in the restaurant.

"I will start with the youngest and work my way to the oldest," she said.

I looked at one table that had children who were no more than ten summers old.

"Okay," I said, and took out enough money to pay the bill and a generous tip. "Johan, do as she says."

Johan and I pushed back our chairs and stood, then walked out of the curry house, heading east.

The woman fell in a step behind us.

"Johan, do you know her?" I whispered.

"The Widow Bride," said Johan. "I saw her once. At Lumenhold."

"I'm flattered that you know me," she said.

As we walked toward the alley that the woman had told us to enter, I tried to remember which alley Prue and Mara were down. I was sure it was one that was farther down the street. *At least Prue is safe.*

The alley was dark except for a door propped open at the far end, spilling yellow light across the cobbles. Someone was standing in the middle of it.

It was Prue. *How did she get here first?*

"You said you were working late, Evan," said Prue. "Is this 'working late'? With HIM?"

Prue pointed at Johan. I looked at Johan, who looked back at me. *What?*

"You said you loved me!" said Prue. *That one's true.*

"I let you into my bed!" said Prue. *That one is... not true yet.*

"I don't care about your lovers' quarrel," said the woman.

"You've lain with her too?" said Prue. "Do you have no shame?"

I saw a shadow shift behind the Widow Bride. *Is that Mara? Don't look at her!*

"This is what I get for going out with a guy who was found in a river, naked!" said Prue. "He can't even haggle and makes weird faces when he eats orc!"

*All true.*

Suddenly, the Widow Bride stumbled forward. Someone had kicked her. She rolled and came up with a knife in each hand. Then Mara appeared out of the darkness.

"Stroya. I should have known you wouldn't die so easily," said the Widow Bride.

"Harna. You look good for your age," said Mara.

"Stroya?" I asked.

Prue took my hand. "Focus, Evan." Then Prue guided Johan and me to the side of the alley.

For a moment nobody moved. The two of them stood on either side of the light, knives low.

Behind me, Johan shifted his feet.

The Widow Bride's eyes flickered to him. Just for a second.

Mara was already moving. I saw her knife go up and the Widow Bride's arm come across to block it, and then I saw Mara's other hand, the one I'd forgotten about.

The Widow Bride made a small sound, surprised more than anything. Then she folded onto the cobbles and didn't get up. Her eyes were still open. I made myself look away.

Mara stood over her, breathing hard. "You always did watch the wrong person," she said quietly.

"So! Who's your friend?" asked Mara.

"Um. This is Johan," I said.

Johan waved at Mara. "They called me the Tide of Death."

"You meet the most interesting people, Evan," said Mara. "Do you really like swimming in the river naked?"

I looked at Prue. "You said that out loud?"

"Mara said she needed a distraction," said Prue. "Besides, it's true."

*Fair point.*

I looked at the body. "Guess I'll get the feet."

Prue and Mara shared a look.

"Do you want me to—" asked Johan.

"No," we all said, maybe a little too loudly.

Prue and Mara grabbed the shoulders and led the way to the river landing. There we laid Harna down on the edge of the landing before pushing her into the river.

Prue came up to me and hugged me. We stood like that for a moment. Then she leaned back and said, "You had me worried."

"I was worried too," I said. "I'm glad you and Mara are okay. And thank you for rescuing us."

"How did you get to that alley before us?" I asked.

"Back door of the bakery," said Prue. "The baker owes Mara a favor."

"And now Evan owes me a favor," said Mara, smiling.

"A man came to the Guild today. He wanted to add something to Plan B's file," said Prue.

"What man?" I asked.

"The same one who came by to read the file before," said Prue. *Fancy Guard!*

"Why would he do that?" I asked.

"I told him only party members can add to a file. He said, 'Oh. Well, never mind,' and walked out." She held out a slip of folded paper. "He forgot this."

I took the paper, opened it, and read it out loud. "Snow soon."
