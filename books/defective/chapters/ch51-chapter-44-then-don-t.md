# Chapter 44: Then Don't

Everyone left to tell their families. I took Johan with me. We agreed to meet back up at the coach just before noon. That left me time while the others talked to their families.

I knew Johan would need a few things for the trip. Then I looked at his clothes. They were threadbare, with holes in places.

"Johan, let's go shopping," I said. Johan looked a bit surprised at that, but followed me. We went to a general store. I asked the shopkeeper to help Johan pick out some work clothes, shoes, and a jacket for cold weather. I found a bedroll, blanket, tinderbox, jerky, and water skin. *I'll need to stop by the Guild and take out some money today.*

We left the store, and my stomach growled loudly. *Oh, yeah. I skipped breakfast.*

"Do you mind if we pick up something to eat?" I asked. "Are you hungry?"

"No. That's fine," said Johan.

We stopped at the café on our way to the coach stop, and I picked up an orc skewer. As I ate it, Johan said, "Hm. You do."

"I do what?" I asked.

"Make a face when you eat orc," said Johan.

We met the others at the coach.

"How did it go?" I asked.

"My mother said something about me sending her back to where she cracked her ribs, then said the air would do her good," said Vagid.

"Pip wants to show them his scar," said Nara, shaking her head. She looked at me. "My father is more suspicious of you now."

"As long as they're safe, I don't care," I said. Nara smiled at that.

"My mom said they'd pack for winter. Evan, you're never getting your cloak back from Katia. She carries it around with her everywhere," said Orrin.

"We'll meet up with you as soon as we finish up here," said Vagid.

Johan stood a little apart in his new jacket. He held out his hand, then stopped. "May I?"

I shook it. "Take care of Nara."

"I believe it will be the other way around," said Johan.

They loaded their gear and climbed in the coach. We waved to them as it pulled away.

I turned to Vagid. "How about we get some lunch and discuss what we have left to do?"

"Café?" asked Vagid.

We found seats at the café. I ordered another set of orc skewers, and Vagid ordered rabbit stew.

"I need to stop at the Guild after lunch to get some money and talk to Prue."

"How do you do it?" asked Vagid.

"Do what?" I asked.

"Make it look so easy to talk to Prue," said Vagid. "I'm terrified of saying the wrong thing to Mara."

"Were you scared when we faced the thing in the berry patch?" I asked.

"I was terrified," said Vagid.

"But you were able to keep focused and do the job," I said.

"That's a job. It's what I've been doing for years," said Vagid.

"Try thinking of this as a job, then. Maybe that will help," I suggested.

"A job..." said Vagid. "I can give it a try."

Our food arrived, and I picked up a skewer and took a bite. *These are really good.*

"You do," said Vagid.

"I know I make a face when I eat orc," I said with a sigh. "But these are really good."

After we finished our lunches, we walked to the Guild. Before we went in, I said, "Prue's line is usually shorter than Mara's, but I'm going to be watching you. If you freeze up again, I'm going to shout your dinner invitation so the whole Guild hears."

I joined Prue's line and Vagid joined Mara's line.

I reached the front of Prue's line first. "Back for more abuse?"

"I needed to take out some money from my account and ask you to dinner again," I said.

"How much will you need?" asked Prue.

"Two or three suns' worth, broken down," I said.

Prue went to the treasurer's desk to get the money. When she came back, she had a medium-sized pouch. She took out two slips and wrote something on each, before sliding the money over the counter along with the slips.

I picked up my things and winked at her before walking over to watch Vagid. He was next in Mara's line. Before he stepped up to speak with Mara, he looked around to see where I was standing.

"Hi, Vagid! It's so good to see you again!" said Mara.

"Hi, Mara," said Vagid. "I've come to ask you to dinner tonight."

Mara's smile didn't change, but I could see her blushing now. "Great! I'll see you here when the Guild closes!" said Mara.

Vagid nodded and came over to where I was standing.

"Well done," I said.

"It worked," said Vagid, "at least so far." He nodded at the slips in my hand. "How about you?"

I opened the first slip. It was my remaining balance.

I opened the other slip, and read it aloud.

> Dinner at my place. Tonight. —P

I smiled like an idiot. Vagid took one look at me and shook his head.

"Let's go talk to Halden," said Vagid.

We walked to Amaranth's chapel in silence.

We saw Halden in the small garden in front of Amaranth's chapel, pulling weeds.

"Halden, are there groups heading to Garnerby to help out?" asked Vagid.

"Yes," said Halden. *I wonder if he defaults to monosyllabic answers?*

"We need to get our families out of town for a while," said Vagid.

Halden stopped gardening and looked up at Vagid, then at me.

"Hmf," he said. "They can go with Amaranth's priests that we're sending. They leave tomorrow at midmorning."

"Thank you," we both said.

"I'll add it to the tab," said Halden, raising one eyebrow.

*I'm never going to be able to pay this tab off.*

We left Halden to his work and went around to each family to let them know when and where they needed to be tomorrow.

We split up to prepare for our evenings. I returned to the chapel. I wiped myself down and changed clothes.

I had some time before I needed to leave, so I walked the half league to the river, to the boulder where Nara had carved the message. I made myself comfortable on a flat rock beside it.

*Tired Guy?* I asked.

*Hello, Evan,* came the tired voice.

*We have a plan, and we're working to make it in time,* I said.

*Is that really what you wanted to talk to me about?*

*No. I'm having dinner with Prue again tonight, and I'm nervous,* I said.

*Why would you be nervous?*

*I haven't told Prue about the choice I might have to make,* I said. *About going back.*

*I see.*

*What would you do in my place?* I asked.

*I cannot decide this for you.*

*I still don't know what I should decide,* I said.

*I know you will figure it out.*

I walked back into town, to the street behind the Guild. This was the door where I'd given Prue the hair clip. It seemed like yesterday. *I don't want my time with her to end.*

I knocked on the door.

After a moment, it opened, and Prue stood there, looking beautiful in a simple dress.

"Well, come in," she said, and led me into the house. It was a one-story house. The main room was a combination of the dining room and kitchen. There was a chair by the fireplace with a small side table. I saw a worn ranger cloak on a peg by the door and a map with pins in it, like people use to say "I've been here." *That's a lot of pins.*

Prue led me to the table, where she already had dinner waiting. She'd set my place beside her, not across from her. *Neither of us would have our backs to the door.*

Dinner was orc curry with something that looked like naan.

"This is impressive," I said.

"Dig in," she said.

We ate in silence, though I caught her smiling when I took my first bite.

After we finished dinner, I helped Prue collect the dishes and clean them.

"Evan," she said. "You've been tense all night. What's wrong?"

I sighed and then filled her in on all the things we had learned. Johan's story and Grace's notes. The Great Reset. The forty-four-day deadline.

"I don't think I mentioned to you that Tired Guy said he might be able to send me back before they fix the barrier between our worlds," I said. "But he stressed that only one person could go back. And now there's Johan, who could also go back."

"Will you?" she asked.

"I don't know," I said. "There's not a lot there for me. I haven't talked to my family in years. I don't have any friends, but it's where I grew up. So I feel like I should."

I paused. "Here, I have a lot of friends. And something that I thought I would never find. Someone who I love so much that it hurts sometimes."

Prue took my hand and held it.

"Now there's Johan. He was a young man when he came here, but he's an old man now. Should I let him go back instead? I just don't know."

"You don't have to decide tonight," she said.

"I'm sorry," I said, and got up. "Good night."

I walked over to the door and put my hand on the latch.

"Evan, stay," she said.

"I don't think that's fair to you," I said.

"I'm not asking you to stay forever, just tonight," she said.

I let go of the door.

The next morning, when I woke up, Prue had already left for the Guild. I got dressed and left for the café to meet Vagid.

He was already sitting down eating when I walked up.

"Morning," he said.

"Hey," I said, and ordered an orc omelet.

"So, how was your dinner with Mara?" I asked.

"Fine," said Vagid.

"Well, did treating it like a job help?" I asked.

"Yeah," he said.

"Okay. I guess I'll just have to ask Prue or Mara for the details," I said, smirking.

"No!" he said. "It went well. We had dinner and talked about stuff. I walked her home. We made plans to have dinner again once we get back."

"That's great news," I said. "I'm happy for you both."

Vagid smiled a little at that. "And how did yours go?"

I paused a moment, trying to find a way to say how I felt. "Better than I could have hoped for. And worse than I could have dreamed."

"Worse?" he asked.

"I have never loved someone so completely as I love Prue. She's everything to me. If I go back, I'll lose that," I said.

Vagid thought about it. "Then don't."

*Is it that simple? Would I have regrets about not going back? Over time, would I resent Prue?*

Vagid stood up, slapped a hand on my shoulder, and said, "Come on. Let's go keep some people safe."
