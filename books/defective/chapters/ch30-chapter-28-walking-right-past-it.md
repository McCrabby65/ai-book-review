# Chapter 28: Walking Right Past It

Just before dawn I got dressed and headed to the Guild training grounds. I arrived as the sun was coming up and saw several other adventurers. All seemed to be younger than me.&#32;

*Great, I'm still the old guy.*

When Stella appeared at the edge of the training grounds, the adventurers lined up in a row in front of her. I joined them on the end. Stella nodded and immediately started a calisthenic routine that would have killed most people half her age and twice her weight, but she seemed fine, never breaking a sweat or showing fatigue. All of the adventurers tried to keep up. When we started the second set, my leg gave out and I went down on a patch of dirt I'd picked out in advance. One by one the others followed. At least I wasn't the first to fall. I was second.

Stella looked around at the groaning adventurers, then said, "Same time tomorrow." As she walked past me, she said, "A lot to unlearn."

*Ugh.*

I hobbled past the others and headed into the Guild. I joined Prue's line and waited my turn.

"Most people at least shower before looking for a job," said Prue when I got to the front of her line.

"Can we talk... in private?" I asked.

I saw Prue's eyes narrow, but otherwise she didn't show any emotion. She turned to Mara and said, "I'll be back in a few minutes."

"Conference room," said Prue as she led me to a small room with a small table and four chairs.

"Well?" asked Prue.

"I'm sorry. I need to tell you some things, and I don't want others knowing," I said. "Last night you mentioned that I look at things like I've never seen them before."

"Yeah, and?" said Prue.

"Well, the reason is... I really haven't seen them before. Because this isn't my world," I said. I held my breath, waiting for her to yell or scream or just walk away.

"Huh. Well, I guess that explains it," said Prue.

*What?*

"Uh. I just told you I'm an Otherborn, and you just go, 'huh'?" I asked.

"I figured you were from somewhere strange. I just thought you'd tell me when you were ready," said Prue.

"You knew?" I asked.

"Evan, you stare at everything like you've never seen it before. And you make funny faces when you eat orc. Like you think you're eating a person," said Prue.

"What powers do you have?" asked Prue.

"None," I said.

"They always have powers," said Prue.

"Yeah. About that—" I said.

"I thought maybe you were hiding them," said Prue.

"I'm very bad at hiding things," I lamented.

"I noticed," said Prue, with a small smile.

"So what are you going to do?" I asked. "Now that you know."

"That depends." Prue tilted her head. "Do you want to hear how long I've known?"

"...Not really," I said.

"Too bad." She held up a finger. "You showed up in town with no family, no hometown, and no money. The registration form had a lot of blank lines."

A second finger. "You don't have any magic. Everyone has a little magic."

A third. "You aren't good at combat. You aren't even good at being bad at combat. You're bad at it in new ways."

"You didn't know what a spark was worth, and you still don't know how to haggle."

"Your writing looks like squiggles."

"And yesterday soldiers asked the Guild for recently registered adventurers." She put her hand down. "I can add, Evan."

After a small pause, she said, "So I've had some time to think about what I'm going to do."

I waited. "And?"

"I'm going to hear the rest of it. You said you needed to tell me some things, and so far all you've told me is that you're from somewhere else."

"I'm looking for clues to stop the summonings," I said.

"How do you know that the summonings are the problem?" asked Prue.

"Um... a god told me," I said. *In for a penny, in for a pound.*

"A god? Why you?" asked Prue.

"I don't know," I said. *You were the one who answered,* said Tired Guy. *I'm just saying.*

"What is their name?" she asked.

"Well, he's a new god and doesn't have a name, so I've just been calling him Tired Guy," I said.

Prue was surprised at that, but then started laughing. "Only you could come up with that name," said Prue.

"Yeah, that's what Vagid said too," I said.

"So that's the plan," I said. "Find out who's doing the summoning and stop them. The problem is, I've run out of clues."

"Well, that sucks," she said.

"How has your day been going?" I asked.

"Well, I was helping Mara with reorganizing the register," she said. "I couldn't find a job slip. I had searched everywhere."

She sighed.

"It turned out to be on the shelves around the door to the records room. I kept going into and out of the records room and walking right past it," she said.

Prue looked at me pointedly and said, "Then my boyfriend told me he was from another world, but otherwise great."

"Yeah, that would suck," I said. *Wait. What was that last part?*

Prue stood there staring at me expectantly, tapping her foot.

"Wait, you have a boyfriend?" I asked.

Prue put her head in her hand and sighed again.

"Did you mean me?" I asked.

"Who else would I mean?" she asked.

"You're a beautiful woman who could have her choice of any of the adventurers..." I said. "You could have anyone you wanted."

"I'm glad you can see my worth. Too bad you don't see your own."

*It was on the shelves around the door. Walking right past it.*

*Isn't that what happened in the archive?* asked Tired Guy.

*You're right,* I replied.

"I think I know where to look next," I said.

"Don't tell me where," said Prue. "If anyone asks, I'd like to be able to say I don't know."

"You're an amazing woman," I said, smiling at her.

"Good. You should remember to say that a lot."

We shared a quick kiss.

"Not bad, but you can do better next time," she said.

I left to find Plan B. We needed to make some plans.
