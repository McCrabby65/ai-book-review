# Lower Lumenhold — Ash

Tobin had breakfast with his wife and son, then walked to his workshop to finish cleaning and repairing a pair of boots he'd started the day before. Since the kobolds were gone, Koren let Ronar play in the yard again. Tobin could hear him through the workshop wall telling a stick about his day.

"What a nice stick," Tobin heard someone say.

Tobin's hands stopped. He set down the boot and reached for the heavy mallet before he even knew he'd moved.

"It's the bestest stick," Tobin heard Ronar say. "Are you here to get your boots fixed?"

"Yes," the voice said. "Is your dad in his shop working?"

A man's voice, not a kobold's rasp. Tobin let out his breath and set the mallet back on the bench.

"Yeah," said Ronar.

Then a few seconds later, "Daddy! A man's here with his boots!"

"Thank you," said the man as he walked through the open shop door. The man was older than most who brought their boots in, but still close to Tobin's age. He was dressed like most soldiers who were off duty, plain pants and linen shirt.

"Good day, sir," said Tobin. "Do you have boots that need cleaning or repair?"

"I do," the man said, and handed an old pair of worn boots over. "These have been with me for a while, but they still have some life left."

"That they do, sir," said Tobin. "I will make sure they live a few more years."

Tobin took the boots and inspected them. They were worn, and the soles were close to wearing through in a few spots, but the uppers were still strong. These boots had plenty of wear left in them. He put the boots on his workbench, then took out an empty tag from a stack. "I'll just need a name to put on them," said Tobin.

"Ash," said the man. "When will I be able to get them back?"

Tobin wrote the name and date on the tag before attaching it to the boots.

"I can have them ready..." said Tobin, looking at the boots waiting in line. "In two weeks."

"Is there any way I can get them back sooner?" asked Ash. "I don't have any spare pairs."

"If you don't mind waiting, I can take care of them now. It will take around two hours," said Tobin. "But it would only be a half-sole job."

"I can wait," said Ash. "A half-sole job is fine."

"Then have a seat," said Tobin, pointing to a nearby stool. "And I'll get to work."

Tobin took out a piece of sole leather, set the first boot down on it, and traced the front of the sole.

"I heard there were kobolds near the village," said Ash. "Is it safe to let your son play in the yard?"

Tobin picked up the second boot to trace its sole, then stopped. "There were." He went back to tracing. "They're gone now. A Guild party cleared them out. Four of them, from Millbrook."

"Four? For a whole kobold den?"

"They brought back the remains of a friend of mine and his daughter." Tobin couldn't keep the sadness out of his voice.

Ash was quiet for a moment. "I'm sorry," he said. He sounded like he meant it.

"I was ashamed that we could only pay them four sparks," said Tobin. "But we didn't have any more money."

Tobin started cutting the new soles from the leather.

"And then they left more on my table than I'd paid them." Tobin shook his head. "Paid me to sleep in my workshop, they said. Who does that?"

"They slept here?" asked Ash. His voice hadn't changed, but he'd stopped watching Tobin's hands.

"Yeah. Even cooked us dinner," said Tobin. "Rabbit stew. They made so much they said they couldn't carry it. My wife still talks about it."

"Just the one night?" asked Ash.

Tobin cut the first half-sole free of the leather and started on the second.

"That time. They came back later, said the Guild wanted them to check the other caves for dens. Camped up in the cave a couple of nights."

Tobin cut away the worn front of the old sole and started pegging the first half-sole into place. Tap, tap, tap.

"The Guild sent them back?" asked Ash.

"Must have." Tobin set a peg and tapped it home. Tap. "Only the one job slip, though." Tap. "Mine."

Ash didn't say anything for a while. When Tobin glanced up, the man was looking at the wall of tagged boots, though Tobin didn't think he was seeing them.

"What were they like?" Ash asked finally.

"Mostly young," said Tobin, as he picked up the second boot to cut away the worn front of the old sole.

"There was a young fella with a bow. Ate everything we put in front of him." Tobin started pegging the second half-sole on. Tap, tap, tap.

"A girl who did magic. Kept the wind off the fire. Ronar called her 'the pretty lady,'" said Tobin, inspecting the new half-soles. He put both boots down on his workbench, then picked up the heel leather and traced the first boot's heel.

"The leader. He was quiet, short, and good with a sword," said Tobin. "He cooked the stew."

Tobin cut out the first heel, then picked up the second boot to trace its heel.

"There was an older one," said Tobin. "Not much of a fighter, I'd guess. But kind. He was the one who told me to ask for them by name if anything else happened."

"Ask for who?" asked Ash.

"Plan B," said Tobin, and he couldn't help smiling. "That's what they call themselves."

Ash didn't move. For a moment, he didn't even seem to breathe. Then he nodded, as if Tobin had told him the weather.

Tobin cut the second heel out and nailed them both into place. He trimmed and polished each boot, then handed them back to Ash.

"There you go," said Tobin. "You should try them on to be sure the feel is right."

Ash pulled them on and stood. He shifted his weight once, heel to toe, the way soldiers did. "They'll do," he said.

"That'll be two sparks," said Tobin.

Ash pressed coins into Tobin's hand. Tobin counted them twice. "Sir, this is too much."

"For the boy," said Ash.

*That's twice now,* Tobin thought.

Then Ash left. Tobin heard him tell Ronar, "Take care of that stick."

Tobin picked up the next pair of boots in line, then set them down again. He looked at the empty stool for a long time.
