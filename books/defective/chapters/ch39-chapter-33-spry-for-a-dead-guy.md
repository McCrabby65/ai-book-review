# Chapter 33: Spry for a Dead Guy

I met with Plan B at the café for breakfast. We agreed to meet back there in the afternoon, once I'd found out what the soldiers wanted. Then I went back to the chapel until close to lunchtime. Prue had said to be late, so I wanted to give the soldiers enough time to clear out.

I walked through Millbrook to the Guild. When I entered, I joined the other people in Prue's line. As soon as I reached the front, Prue called over to Mara, "I'm taking lunch." She grabbed my hand and we walked out of the Guild. Mara waved at us with her whole arm. The fox pin was still in her hair.

We walked around to the training ground. Prue sat down on the benches, and I sat across from her, holding her hands.

"The soldiers came. They took the registrant list. Your name was on it," said Prue.

"I know. It's okay," I said. "They had to find out at some point."

"Then an older guy came in and asked to see Plan B's file," Prue said, almost like an apology. "I had to give it to him."

"What did he look like?" I asked.

"He was probably in his early forties. He had salt and pepper hair. He was dressed as a commoner, but stood like someone who was used to giving orders."

An image flashed through my mind, a man in nice armor, a cloak and perfect posture. *Fancy Guard.*

My realization must have shown on my face, because Prue gripped my hand harder for a second. "Who was it?"

"Someone from when I first arrived, I think," I said. "I can't be sure without seeing him and that would be very dangerous."

"I'm sorry, Evan," said Prue.

"No. You have nothing to be sorry about," I said. "I knew this would happen eventually."

I thought for a second. "Did he take the file?"

"No. Even if someone asks for the file, all they can do is read it in the Guild, or ask that it be copied."

"Did he ask for a copy?"

"No." Prue frowned. "He read the whole thing, closed it, and left it on the table."

My copy of Thirteen's record was in that file. In my handwriting.

*Someone who was there when I was summoned just read* *my death certificate. And he didn't take it with him.*

"Can I take something out of our file?"

"It's your file," said Prue. "You can take out whatever you want."

"Do I need Cuthbert for that?"

"No." Prue stood up and brushed off her skirt. "You need me. I have the key to the records room."

"I hope I have enough to pay for that privilege," I said.

"You don't," said Prue, smiling. "But you can haggle me down later."

We went back into the Guild and Prue led me to the records room. She pulled out Plan B's file and set it down for me on the counter. I flipped through all of the documents until finally I reached the last one. There I saw my handwriting on the copy I had made of Thirteen's record. I picked it up, folded it and put it in my pocket.

"That's all I need," I said. "You can put the rest back."

Prue picked up the folder and put it away. "What was on that paper you took?"

"My death certificate," I said.

"You're pretty spry for a dead guy," said Prue.

"I've been told I have a good complexion."

"You'll tell me the rest tonight," said Prue. It wasn't a question.

"Tonight," I agreed. "I'll be by at closing time."

Prue went back to work, and I started walking to the café.

On the way, I kept my hand over my pocket.

*Tired Guy?*

*Hello, Evan, *said a tired voice.

*The Fancy Guard from my summoning ritual came to the Guild and read all of the notes I had collected.* *He read my record**, and he left it. Why would he leave it?*

*Perhaps he did not want to be found carrying it*.

*That's not comforting.*

*It was not meant to be.*

I reached the café and joined Plan B. Orrin was eating a sandwich of some kind. Vagid was drinking an ale. Nara was sipping tea.

"The soldiers picked up the registrant list," I said. "Then someone else showed up and asked to see our file."

Orrin stopped chewing for a second. "Wemgl," he said.

"Not with your mouth full," chided Nara.

"Is Mara okay?" asked Vagid. "And Prue, of course."

"They're fine," I said. "I think the person who asked for the file was someone who was there when I arrived."

"Did they take anything?" asked Nara.

"No. They went through the file though," I said. "If they can only read the Hallish parts, then they might figure out that I'm not from around here."

"You're not from around anywhere," said Orrin, laughing.

I took the folded page out of my pocket and smoothed it flat on the table.

"Thirteen, and four hundred," said Orrin. "What's that supposed to mean?"

"It's my death certificate. My copy, anyway. The original's in Hallish," I said.

"So what does it say?" asked Orrin.

I read it to them. Then Vagid said, "If the man who read it is the one who wrote the original, he knows it's you. And he knows you're alive."

Orrin put his sandwich down and pushed the plate away.

"Then we'd better move faster," said Vagid. "We have another place to search: Greywater Keep."

"Oh good," said Orrin, reaching for his sandwich again. "Vagid loves old keeps."

"How long will it take to get there?" I asked.

"By coach it's a three-day journey," said Vagid. "And the cost is more because of that."

"I won't need another loan," I said. "I've been saving my money."

"Can we leave in the morning?" I asked. "I'm having dinner with Prue again."

"Again?" asked Orrin. "At this rate you'll be marrying her next."

"Did Vagid tell you about his dinner with Mara?" I asked.

Orrin's head swung toward Vagid like a hound's. "What dinner?"

Vagid's ears went red. "It was a meal. People eat meals."

"He held her hand," I said. "And he said 'Eep.'"

"I did not say 'Eep.'"

Nara sipped her tea. "You did. Mara told me this morning."

Orrin leaned back, delighted. "Eep," he said, to no one in particular.

"We leave on the noon coach tomorrow," said Vagid, in the voice of a man ending a conversation. "Everyone pack tonight."

"Eep," said Orrin.

I left them to it. The Guild would be closing soon, and I had promised Prue the rest of the story.

*Tonight, I tell her everything.*
