# Chapter 40: Not for Water

"It was when I saw it. And that was only a few weeks ago, their time," I said.

"Thank you," said Johan in English. The pronunciation was off, like he hadn't spoken English in decades.

Johan followed my gaze and turned around. He saw Nara standing in the doorway. "I said the rest stay put."

"You killed dozens of people in Garnerby. And you injured dozens. My brother Pip has his arm bandaged from a four-inch gash. Vagid's mother, Ruta, had several ribs cracked. Orrin's little sister, Katia, was blue with cold in Evan's cloak. A little boy, not more than seven summers old, was crushed under a wagon. He will never grow up."

Johan stared at the floor. "What was his name?"

"Tam," said Nara quietly.

"Tam," whispered Johan.

"Can the rest of my party come up?" I asked. "They are probably worried about us."

"Yes," said Johan.

"And the gap?" I asked.

"It will hold," said Johan.

I walked back to the window and yelled, "Vagid, Orrin, come on up. We're just talking!"

I watched them from the window, two dark shapes on a silver lake. Vagid went first. He tested the water with one foot, then crossed like a speed walker. Orrin gave the water a light test, then strolled across the gap admiring the keep.

A moment later they appeared in the doorway.

"Wow," said Orrin. "These are some seriously amazing models."

Orrin started to reach for one.

"Don't," I said.

Vagid looked around the room. He stayed near Nara.

"Johan, we've learned a lot about the summons, but we haven't found where the ritual is held," I said. "All we know is that it's somewhere around Thornspire."

"There is a cave at the bottom of the eastern side," said Johan. "It leads to a circular domed chamber. There are symbols on the floor in tarnished silver."

Johan paused. "It takes nine months to gather all the people for the sacrifices. If they had enough people, they could cut it down to six months."

"How did you find all of this out?" I asked.

"Silas wanted me to know. He made me go to a ritual. Even after I arrived, they kept trying to summon more."

"Were you at my summoning?" I asked.

"No, I was here. It's peaceful here."

Johan looked at me. "Silas was already gathering when I left. He never waits longer than he has to."

I thought about it. If Silas had started the day I was thrown away, then they could start the next ritual about six months later. That means we only have three months left, probably less.

"Johan, can you excuse us for a minute?" I asked. "I want to talk something over with my party. We'll be right outside the doorway."

Johan nodded.

Plan B and I went to the stairway.

"I want to ask Johan to come back to Millbrook with us," I said.

"Agreed," said Vagid. Orrin shrugged.

"Absolutely not!" said Nara. "You want me to bring him closer to Pip, Ruta, and Katia? So that he can try again?"

"I can't deny that Johan is dangerous, but we can't leave him for others to use," I said.

"If we leave him, and Silas finds him, he'll point him at the next town," said Vagid. "He comes with us."

Nara scowled at Vagid and me. But after a minute, she said, "Fine! He doesn't go near my family."

I nodded.

We went back into the room, and I approached Johan. "We would like you to come with us. Back to Millbrook," I said.

"Why?" asked Johan.

"Frankly, you're too dangerous to leave here," I said. "Plus, there are more things that you and I need to talk about."

He seemed to be weighing the options. Then he nodded.

"There are some conditions, though," I said. "Don't use any magic without checking with us first."

"Agreed," said Johan. "I've spent forty years doing what I'm told. I can manage."

"Stay away from my family," said Nara from the doorway.

Johan turned to the doorway. "I know their names. I don't know their faces. I'll stay away from them."

"Why don't you go pack your things for the trip," I said. "Then we can head back to the coach pickup spot."

He walked over to the windowsill, then reached up to a shelf and took down a ledger. Turning, he said, "I have everything I need."

"A ledger?" I asked. "Don't you need some personal things?"

Johan opened the ledger and held it up for us to see. It was an old kingdom ledger. Every page was covered in drawings, the columns lost under the lines. The last page was a view from his windowsill. It was extremely detailed and beautiful.

"This is all I need."

I nodded, then we turned to leave. I saw Johan tentatively reach out to the model of Lindmoras Livs before turning and following us out of the keep.

We crossed the gap, then walked by moonlight to the cart track. The sky was slowly brightening.

As the sun broke over the horizon, Johan stopped. He stood in the middle of the track and watched it come up, the light spreading gold across the treetops.

We stopped with him. Nobody said anything.

"When did you last watch one?" I asked.

"I don't remember," said Johan.

After a few minutes, he turned to us. His eyes were wet, but his face was calmer than I'd seen it. Then he turned and continued along the trail.

*Tired Guy?* I said.

*Evan, I am glad that you are well,* came a tired voice.

*Alive anyway,* I said. *We found Twelve, Johan Nilsson.*

*I have felt him for a long time.* A pause. *I did not know his name.*

*He's coming back to Millbrook with us,* I said. *There but for the grace of—well, you, really—go I.*

*I'd say we're saving each other.* His voice seemed amused.

*Evan, stop for a moment. I have important news.*

We were back at the place where the coach had dropped us, so I said, "Let's rest until the coach comes by." I sat on the ground and rested on my pack.

*Okay,* I said. *What's the news?*

*Korrath and Sallow are pushing for a preemptive reset.* Then more quietly: *Amaranth is leaning toward the reset too, after Garnerby.*

"Crap!" I said out loud.

"Evan, what's wrong?" asked Nara.

*What can I do to help?* I asked.

*Show them it can end. If the chamber is destroyed, they cannot argue that the summonings will continue.*

"How? How can we destroy it?" I asked.

"Evan!" said Vagid. "What is wrong?"

I ignored them. This was too important. But all I heard was silence.

The slap wasn't very hard, but it did get my attention.

I focused on Vagid standing in front of me, getting ready for another slap.

"Wait," I said. "I was talking to Tired Guy."

Vagid lowered his hand. "And it wasn't good news."

"No," I said.

"Who is Tired Guy?" asked Johan.

"Evan talks to a god," said Orrin. "All the time."

"A god?" said Johan. "I didn't think they talked to people."

"Most don't, but this is a new god and he's been talking to Evan since he arrived," said Vagid.

"Sorry about the slap," he added.

"Well, don't keep us waiting," said Nara. "What did he tell you?"

"The other gods are thinking about doing something... drastic," I said. "It's called the Great Reset."

"Reset what?" asked Vagid.

"Humanity," I said.

"What happens to everyone, our families?" asked Nara.

"The way it was explained to me was, all humans, animals, and gods would die. Only monsters would survive. Something about their cores."

"Why would the gods choose to do that?" asked Johan.

"Because of the summonings," I said. "Every one of them tears the wall between this world and Earth. One more, and both worlds break." I paused. "The last time this happened, the gods reset everything. They're thinking about doing it again."

Johan looked down at his hands.

"At the end, you said, 'How can we destroy it?'" said Orrin. "What?"

"The chamber," I said. "Tired Guy said that if we can prove that no more summonings would happen by destroying the chamber, then the other gods wouldn't try to reset everything."

"Did he tell you how we should destroy the chamber?" asked Vagid.

"No. Either he doesn't know, or it's something that we need to figure out for ourselves," I said.

"I know how," said Johan quietly.

Everyone turned to him. He was still looking at his hands.

"One of the others left instructions. The sixth one, a woman named Grace. She carved them into the stone behind the chamber, in English. The guards thought it was decoration." He paused. "I found them years ago."

"What did they say?" I asked.

"That breaking the floor isn't enough. The pattern remembers. The stones would have to be ground down to sand, every one of them, or it could be made to work again."

"You've known this for years?" asked Nara.

Johan finally looked up. "I thought the chamber was my only way home."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"Grinding stone to sand," said Vagid. "That would take years."

"Not for water," I said slowly.

Johan didn't look away this time. "No," he said. "Not for water."
