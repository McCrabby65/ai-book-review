# Chapter 36: River Guy

We made our way toward the high ground. Survivors huddled around a few campfires, calling names into the dark.

We got to the edge of the survivor camp and the rest of us turned to Vagid. He stood there for a moment, gathering his thoughts.

"Nara, cast Search every few minutes. Tell the others where you hear the heartbeats. Don't go into the water."

"Orrin, find our families. All of them. Check if there are any healers."

"I'm going to coordinate with the people searching," said Vagid. "Evan, you look for survivors."

I nodded, then looked around. *This is where the water hit, but it pushes** everything downstream. That's where I need to look. *I started in that direction.

As I walked, I could hear Vagid giving orders to others who had approached him to help.

It kept echoing through my mind. *This is my fault.*

*Of course I'm the one looking for survivors. I'm the reason there are survivors to look for.*

I kept walking downstream. Mara had warned me. The files list next of kin. And I thought they would be *s**afer there than here.*

*I keep causing so much death and destruction. Have I done any good?*

*Maybe... maybe I should just go away.*

As I walked I saw ribbons in the water held in place by branches. I saw a waterlogged pillow trapped under a branch. Part of a tent was stuck high up in a tree. Then I saw a small shoe. A child's shoe. I kept walking down the debris trail. *Please. Something. Anything. Please let me be useful.*

I saw a man's body impaled on a branch. He was past helping. I kept looking. *Please.* It was starting to get darker, and I hadn't found anything. Then I saw a small hand sticking out from the side of a tree. I took several deep breaths. Then I made my way over to the tree. The small hand was dirty and had small cuts on it. I looked around the tree and saw that the child was Katia. Orrin's little sister. She looked dead. I felt her wrist for a pulse. Nothing. I slowly moved her away from the tree. She was very cold. I put my ear to her chest and waited. *Please, Tired Guy.* No sound. I pulled back. *My fault. All my fault. Why am I even here?*

I held Katia in my lap, crying. Then, as if Tired Guy had heard me, she shivered.

*H**ypothermia!*

What was it they said in the kayaking class? *College. A lot of useless information.* Get out of the water. Get dry. Get out of any wind. Get in something dry. Warm slowly and keep dry. Don't rub their extremities.

I picked up Katia and started moving away from the wet to find some dry wood for a fire. Drier wood would be on higher ground, so that's where I carried Katia. But even on the higher ground the wood was wet. *What should I do? Just because the outside is wet doesn't mean the inside is wet.* At least I hoped that was the case. But before that, I needed to get Katia out of her wet things and wrapped in whatever I had that was dry.

I set Katia down and looked through my pack. My cloak was on top, so I took it out and laid it out on a dry patch. Then I held Katia while I took off her wet outer layers. *Sorry, Katia.*

Once I had all of her wet things off, I wrapped her in my dry cloak like a mummy. I looked around for any large pieces of wood I could find. Then I took my sword out and hacked them until the wet parts were cut off. I needed tinder too. I looked around for some dry pieces of wood that had splintered and found a few. I used my sword to scrape off shavings of dry wood for the tinder. I had a good-sized pile of shavings so I took out my tinderbox and tried to light the shavings. They smoked and I blew on them, but they wouldn't catch. I tried again with more shavings. Still nothing. *What am I doing wrong?* I took a deep breath, and a moment to think. I had everything to start the fire but it wasn't starting. *Maybe I'm rushing it.* I tried the steps again, but more methodically. More tinder. Then the spark from the tinderbox. Slow, steady breaths to get the tinder to catch.

Finally, the fire caught. A tiny ember in the shavings. I kept giving it air and feeding it twigs and shavings. Then slowly it grew in size. After fifteen minutes I had a small fire and was able to add some of the smaller pieces of wood to it. I took Katia in my lap and sat with her like that, feeding the fire and waiting.

I don't remember how long I sat like that. When the wood got low, I would get up and get more, then sit back down. I kept praying to Tired Guy to let her make it.

"River Guy?" said Katia. She sounded tired and sleepy. I started crying again. *Thank you!*

"Hi, Katia," I said. "I'm glad you woke up. You had me really worried."

"Sorry," she said, then fell asleep.

*I can't stay here with Katia. She needs a healer. She needs someone other than me.*

I put out the fire, put on my pack, and picked up Katia. Then I started back.

After a long time—I don't remember how long—I heard a voice upstream.

Someone was calling her name. Orrin. His voice cracked on it, like he'd been shouting for hours.

Katia's hand found my sleeve and held on.

I walked faster.
