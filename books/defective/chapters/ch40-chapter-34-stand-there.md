# Chapter 34: Stand There

I got to the Guild just before closing. For once, I was early.

"Hi, Evan! I'll see you tomorrow!" said Mara on her way out the door. "Oh. Wait. I left my bag in the back."

Mara disappeared into the back.

I saw Prue exit the records room and lock the door. She took a moment to double-check, then triple-check that the door was locked.

*A bit OCD there.*

"Ready?" asked Prue as she came over.

"Where to tonight?" I asked. "Not curry, but I'm not too familiar with any other places."

"Let's head to the town center and check out our options."

I offered Prue my arm, which she took, and we walked to the town center.

We passed several open-air places. I suggested one of them, but Prue didn't like the open-air "thing." So we kept looking.

Eventually, we came to a small shop that offered steaks of various kinds. The sign out front was just a picture of a steak.

Prue led me to a table near the back wall and took the chair facing the door.

*She always does that.*

The steak shop was not very full, but there were enough people that the noise would cover most of our conversation.

I sat next to Prue so that it would be easier to talk.

I told her everything. Rachel Lin, an Otherborn summoned a hundred and seventy-five years before me. Her notes, and the archive they pointed to. The kobold tunnel under the castle. Rachel's death report, and my own: *defective, disposed of*. The papers we took, and where we'd hidden them.

Prue's knife stopped halfway through her steak.

"You put stolen royal documents in a Guild file."

"Cuthbert filed them. He doesn't read anything."

"*I* read things, Evan."

Then the second trip. The drawings. The man who wasn't there until he was. Orrin. "He called me *Ruiner*."

She was quiet for a long time. "And the man who read your file today?"

"Probably knows all of it."

"Then you're leaving."

"Tomorrow. We're going—"

She put a finger on my lips. "Not here."

We finished our dinner in silence. When we were ready to leave, I offered Prue my hand. She looked at me for a second, then took it.

We started the walk back to her house. Prue led me a different way than before. We went down smaller, quieter streets.

"We take the noon coach," I said. "I know you don't want to know where we're going."

"Greywater Keep," said Prue. "Obviously."

"Yeah," I said. "Something was sent there. We need to find it and figure out why it's important."

Prue was quiet for a few steps, then said, "And you don't know when you're coming back?"

"No."

I was lost in my thoughts when Prue pulled me to a stop. I looked around and noticed we were in an alley.

"Why'd we stop?" I asked.

Prue didn't say anything, but she was staring at something farther down the alley. I looked and noticed someone in dark clothes step out of a shadow.

"Give me all of your money," said a gruff voice. "Or I'll have to hurt you."

I stepped in front of Prue. "How much do you want? I can haggle."

The man kept walking slowly toward us.

I took out the few rays I had and tossed them toward the man. He didn't even try to catch them, just kept walking. I watched him draw a knife from behind his back.

*The hell is this?*

Prue hadn't moved. "Evan, you need to move."

"Prue, run. I'll hold him off," I said, and put up my fists. The man just kept walking toward us. *Apparently, I'm not** a threat. Rude, but accurate.*

I looked around for any kind of weapon and saw an old chair. So I grabbed it and held it in front of me, like a lion tamer.

"Evan." I felt Prue's hand on my shoulder, and suddenly I was shoved aside. "Stand there."

*What is going on?*

The man got within ten feet, then leaped at Prue, who deftly redirected his knife and slammed her elbow into the man's face, then followed up with a knee to the groin.

The man backed off for a second. I saw Prue in a fighting stance that I knew. I had been failing at it every morning at sunrise.

The man aimed a kick at her stomach. Prue spun, deflected the kick, and landed one of her own to the man's head. *Who is this woman and what has she done with Prue?*

*She does not require your assistance,* said a tired voice.

*I noticed.*

*I am merely confirming.*

The man was staggered by the kick to the head. He seemed to rethink his career choices and ran for the open end of the alley. Prue looked like she was about to run after him when someone stepped out of the shadows in front of the man. He stopped and raised his knife. It didn't matter. The other person closed in, and the man was dead in seconds. I hadn't even seen what they did. It was too fast.

The figure started walking toward us and said, "Oh good, you're both okay!"

"Mara?" I asked. *Who are these women?*

"Hi, Evan! Hi, Prue!" said Mara. "You did good, but you shouldn't let them get away."

"I had to keep an eye on this one," said Prue, nodding at me. "Besides, you got him."

"Evan, close your mouth. It makes you look silly," said Mara.

I had been standing there with my mouth open, still holding the chair like a lion tamer.

"I must be dreaming," I said. "Or maybe I hit my head or something."

"What kind of dreams do you have?" asked Mara. "Are you a pervert?"

"I'm sorry. I just watched the woman I love beat up a man, and the woman my friend loves kill him," I said as quietly as I could manage. "I might need a minute to adjust."

"Did Vagid say he loves me?" asked Mara.

"You love me?" asked Prue.

I stared at them, probably with my mouth open again.

Prue reached out her hand and lightly closed my mouth.

"He didn't say it, but it's clear to everyone," I told Mara.

I turned to Prue. "Yes, I do love you. But I don't know if I deserve you, and everything that's messed up about me is just putting you in danger."

Prue looked at me for a moment. "I think I'll manage."

"I know. But I want to hear him say it," said Mara. "A girl just wants to be told that she's loved."

"What now?" I asked.

"Come on. We need to clean up," said Prue.

"We'll get the shoulders if you get the feet," said Mara.

"Hired help," she added, checking his hands. "No magic. Whoever sent him will send someone better."

"What about the blood in the alley?" I asked. "Won't someone notice it?"

"There's always blood in alleys, if not from muggings, from dogs fighting," said Prue. "Most people don't even notice."

"Besides, there isn't any blood," said Mara. "I snapped his neck."

"What about the chair?" I asked.

Prue walked up to me, took my face in her hands, and kissed me.

"Evan, we need to focus on getting rid of the body," said Prue.

"Okay," I said as I took his feet.

Prue and Mara led the way to one of the many landings under a bridge. We set the body down by the edge of the landing, then just tipped it into the river.

*Someone once** tipped me into a river like that.*

Sitting there on the landing, I looked at the two beautiful women with me. "What are you two?"

Prue looked at me and sighed. "I used to be a top-ranked ranger. I retired."

"I used to do this for the kingdom. Then I died. It was very relaxing," said Mara. "I highly recommend it."

"Wait, you what?" I asked Mara.

"Died," said Mara, like she was explaining it to a toddler.

"But you both know about each other? How?"

"We're friends, Evan," said Prue. "We both train with Stella in her afternoon class."

*Afternoon class. For receptionists. I actually thought that.*

"It's for her intermediate students," said Mara. "We hope to get to her advanced class, which is only on weekends."

"He wasn't a mugger," I said. "He came for you two. Because of me. Because of what's in our file."

"He came because we keep good files," said Prue.

"Right, he came because we're good at our jobs," said Mara.

"Evan," said Prue. "I like working the desk. I don't want to go back to being a ranger."

"Okay," I said. "You should do whatever makes you happy."

"Orrin can never know about my past," said Prue. "Evan, this is important."

"I will take it to my grave," I said. "But why?"

"He'd want lessons, and I can't stand to be a teacher."

"And once Vagid says he loves me, I may tell him. But under no circumstances can you," said Mara. "I need you to promise, Evan."

"I promise never to mention either of them again," I said.

We said good night to Mara and continued our walk to Prue's house.

When we got to her door, we stopped.

I held Prue in my arms and looked into her eyes. "I love you." *I didn't think I could after Katie.*

"Come back by the time the advanced class starts. I'm not explaining your absence to Stella," said Prue.

I nodded, let go of her, and started to leave.

"Evan." She held onto my collar. "I love you too. Try not to die again."

Then she kissed me. It was a long one.

When we parted, I looked at Prue and said, "You definitely get top marks."

"Of course," said Prue, very pleased with herself. "You're getting there."

From somewhere in the dark down the street, Mara called, "See you at the coach!"
