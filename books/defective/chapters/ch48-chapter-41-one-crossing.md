# Chapter 41: One Crossing

Near noon, the coach came through. We each paid our fare, plus a cushion. Vagid paid for Johan and handed him a cushion. Johan looked at the cushion like he didn't know what it was for.

I climbed in with my cushion, fluffed it, placed it on the seat with both hands, sat down, and let out a long, satisfied sigh. The only other passenger stared at me. I was sitting next to him.

Orrin and Vagid sat across from me. Nara got into the coach and joined Orrin and Vagid.

Johan stood just outside the coach, waiting.

"Johan, come sit next to me," I said.

Johan nodded, then climbed into the coach, fluffed his cushion, placed it on the seat with both hands, sat down, and let out a long, satisfied sigh. Then he looked over at me, and I nodded.

Nara, who was across from Johan, suddenly said, "Vagid, switch with me," and moved to the far end, across from the other passenger.

Nothing changed until the first town, where the other passenger got off. Once we were moving again, I turned to Johan.

"Here's what we know," I said. "Silas's promise to send you back with no time passed was never possible. Tired Guy says there are only two ways to go back: a reverse summoning or reincarnation."

"What is the difference?" asked Orrin. "They sound like the same thing."

"A reverse summoning is when you go back as you currently are," I said.

"As I am?" asked Johan.

"Yes."

Johan looked down at his hands. They were an old man's hands.

"You keep your memories," I said.

"Does it need sacrifices?" asked Johan.

"No. When humans do a summoning, the magic does the crossing," I said. "The sacrifices pay for the abilities."

"How many?" asked Johan.

"For my summoning?" I asked.

"For mine," said Johan.

"I don't know. We never found your record," I said.

Johan was quiet for a long time. "And reincarnation?"

"You would start over in a new body as a baby," I said. "You wouldn't remember anything and have no guarantees on how your life would go."

"So why wouldn't everyone choose the reverse summoning?" asked Orrin.

"There can only be one more crossing," I said. "It has to be the gods', and the wall has to be sealed in the same act. If Silas gets his summoning first, both worlds break."

"But if the gods do it, both worlds are safe?" asked Johan.

"Yes," I said. "But no one will ever be able to cross again."

"So Tired Guy can do all of this?" asked Nara.

"No. Not alone. It will take all the gods working together to send someone back and fix the wall," I said.

"The same gods that want to reset everything," said Vagid.

"Yeah," I said.

"Where did Tired Guy come from?" asked Johan. "I thought there were only three gods."

"He was born from the suffering of the summoned," I said.

Nara stared at me, then frowned. "So their suffering was that great? Greater than the people here who died in Garnerby?"

"The people in Garnerby who were hurt or killed already have a god—Amaranth," I said. "The Otherborn had no god looking out for them. That's why."

I rubbed the scars on my arm. I could see tears in her eyes as she looked at me.

"Then some of him is me," said Johan.

"Some of him already is," I said. "But not your memories. Not yet."

Johan turned to look at me.

"When an Otherborn dies," I said, "their soul goes to Tired Guy, and he sees everything they knew."

Johan looked out the window.

"So one day," said Johan.

"Yes. One day that will happen," I said. "He only feels the living. He said he sensed you for decades but didn't know your name."

"And what do you get?" asked Johan.

"A way home. Maybe," I said.

"Silas promised me that too," said Johan.

"Tired Guy never promised me that he could send me home," I said, "only that it might be possible. And he told me that time keeps passing back home. Silas told you it didn't."

"One crossing," said Johan. "One person."

"Yes."

"Will you take it?" asked Johan.

I stared at Johan for a moment, then looked across the coach. Nara was watching me. *Would I?*

"You should take it," said Johan. "You didn't drown anyone."

"I don't know what I'd have done with a power I didn't ask for and forty years of orders," I said. "Neither do you."

"But you didn't," said Nara.

I sighed and looked at the floor of the coach. *She's right and wrong.*

Nobody said much for the rest of the day. At dusk, the coach stopped at an inn. We ate in the common room and then went to our room.

I put Johan by the wall and took the spot next to him, to keep him and Nara apart. Nara watched us as we prepared for bed but didn't say anything.

The next morning, we had breakfast in the common room, then got in the coach. We had it to ourselves again.

"We need the actual date of the next summoning," I said.

*Fancy Guard would know.*

"We could try Thornspire," said Orrin. "Look to see if they are setting up."

"That won't work," said Nara. "We need to know before the date. Watching Thornspire would only tell us once they start."

"What if we keep a watch on Lumenhold?" said Vagid. "That would tell us before they start the ritual."

"When they move the prisoners from the cells, you have days. Not weeks," Johan said, not looking at anyone.

"Then we follow the food. If they have hundreds of prisoners, they'll need a lot. Maybe we can track down a timeline of when extra food is ordered for Lumenhold?" said Vagid.

"That seems risky. Asking around about Lumenhold could get us noticed," I said.

"So what? We guess?" asked Vagid.

None of us had any answers, so we continued in silence. As evening came, the coach stopped at another inn. We had a delicious stew of orc and vegetables. *It really does taste like pork.*

Then we turned in for the night. I repeated the sleeping order from the night before.

We left early the next morning. Nara was sitting opposite me and seemed to be napping. Vagid was beside her and watching the countryside pass by. Orrin was sleeping on one side of Johan, and I was on the other. Johan was working on a new drawing, but it wasn't far enough along for me to tell what it was.

Before the coach got close to Garnerby, we got out. There were still watchers in the area, and we needed to be cautious.

Orrin led us through the forest using game trails.

After a few hours of walking, we stopped to rest. I saw Orrin disappear behind a tree. It looked like he was going to circle behind Johan.

Johan was drawing in his ledger while we rested. Without looking up, he said, "Good afternoon, Orrin."

Orrin stepped out from behind the tree that Johan was leaning against, deflated. "How?"

"Humans are mostly water," Johan said. "I know where water is."

*Sixty percent water, but who's counting?*

Nara looked over from where she was resting. "Can you feel them from here? The people in Garnerby?"

Johan stopped drawing for a second. "Yes." Then he went back to his drawing.

Nara's hand drifted to the knife at her belt, and stayed there.

We resumed our trek. An hour later we crested the ridge above Garnerby.

The water was gone. A brown line ran across every wall in town, all the same height. The festival grounds were a field of mud, and a few ribbons still hung in the trees. People were shoveling mud out of their houses. On the high ground past the town, there was a row of fresh graves.

We'd seen it under water. Nobody wanted to look at it like this for long. We kept walking.

It was a moment before we realized that Johan had stopped just over the ridge. We turned back and saw him taking in the scene. He looked at the valley from one end to the other, slowly, like he was counting.

"Where?" he asked.

Without saying anything, Nara pointed to a wagon that had been crushed against a building. Someone had pulled it free since.

We kept to the tree line above the town. The trail ran past the graves. A man and a woman were kneeling at the smallest one.

Nara stopped. "Tam's parents," she said. Then she walked out of the trees and hugged them.

Johan watched silently.

When Nara returned, we continued to the other side of town. It was too late to catch the coach to Millbrook, so we camped out in the forest.

We made camp. I went to collect the firewood, and Orrin went to hunt.

When Orrin came back with a small boar, the fire was ready. While Vagid cooked the boar, I passed by Johan.

He was drawing a small boy, waving. Underneath, he'd written "Tam."

Nara was walking past, but stopped when she saw the picture. She watched silently for a moment. "His hair curled more."

Johan made changes and looked up. She nodded and then continued on.

When we finished dinner, we agreed on our normal watch rotation: me, Nara, Orrin, then Vagid.

Johan didn't have a bedroll, so I lent him the spare blanket that I'd bought after Katia kept my cloak.

When my watch was over, I fell into my bedroll.

The next morning, we walked to the coach stop. We expected the coach to arrive around lunchtime, but it didn't arrive until early afternoon.

"Bad roads," was all the teamster said. We loaded our gear, then got in the coach.

It was dark by the time the coach rolled into Millbrook. Nara was gone before the wheels stopped. Vagid went to check on his mother, and Orrin went to see Katia.

"Breakfast at the café," Vagid called back.

That left Johan, standing by the coach with his ledger, waiting.

"Come on," I said. "I know a good place to eat."
