# Chapter 38: One Chance

The next morning, we helped the families gather their things.

"You don't have to go," Lida said, holding Orrin's arm. Rafe nodded.

"Mom. Leave Orrin alone." Katia pulled the cloak tighter around her shoulders. My cloak. "Go get him, Orrin."

Orrin kissed the top of his sister's head, then gave his parents hugs.

I saw Vagid shaking his head as Ruta said, "Go. Don't let your poor mother keep you."

"Okay, Mom," said Vagid, and gave his mother a kiss and his father a hug.

Nara hugged her parents, then Pip, who held up his arm again to show off the bandage.

We started walking along the road. Then I heard Pip yell, "Pain is temporary, glory is forever, and chicks dig scars."

I looked at Nara, who was losing a fight not to smile.

Vagid said we should walk along the road until our families were out of sight. Then we could cut through the forest to avoid the area that Fancy Guard mentioned. Orrin assured us that he could find trails through the woods.

After a couple of minutes, Orrin veered off the road toward the forest. We kept to the forest for the rest of the day. That evening we didn't light a fire because Fancy Guard's watchers could be anywhere. Dinner was just jerky and bread. Orrin took first watch and complained about the jerky.

By midmorning, Orrin led us to a small town that Vagid said would have a coach stop.

We were able to arrange for a coach toward Greywater Keep, but we had to pay a second time. Three rays each. Again. *So much for not needing another loan.*

Once we were on the road again, I closed my eyes.

*Tired Guy?* I asked.

*Evan,* came a voice that sounded much more tired than usual.

*Are you okay?*

*Many came to me. I helped the others send them on.* A pause. *Amaranth was there. It was her festival. I have never seen her like that.*

*I'm sorry.*

A pause. *For what?*

*All those people came to you because of me. He flooded that town to get to me.*

*Did you call the water**?*

*No, but—*

*Did you choose where they built Garnerby?** **Did you summon the man who did?* Another pause, longer. *You did not do this, Evan.*

*It feels like I did.*

*I know.* The voice softened. *It's what makes you worth talking to.* Then, quieter: *Remember that he did not choose to be here either. They rarely choose what they are made to do.*

*I will. And please give** Amaranth my condolences.*

A pause. *I will.*

At dusk, the coach stopped at a small town. We ate in the common room in silence. Then we went to our room, which turned out not to be silent.

"I don't trust him," said Nara. "How can you? After the things he did to you?"

"He didn't torture me—that was someone else," I said quietly. "He walked out when the knife came out. He's not a good man. But he wasn't the one holding the knife."

"He left? That's your proof?" said Nara, sneering.

I sighed. "He came and helped rescue people. That should count for something."

Nara turned to Vagid. "You saw what they did to Evan. What they did to our families. Can you forgive them?"

"No," said Vagid. "But I don't need to forgive them to trust Evan."

*You may not after this.*

"I want to talk to Twelve," I said. "Before anyone does anything else."

I expected Nara to be upset. What I didn't expect was the slap. It was loud in the small room. For a second, I couldn't think.

Nara was staring at her hand like it belonged to someone else. "Evan, I'm..."

I stepped forward and pulled her into a hug. She cried on my shoulder, and we stayed like that for several minutes.

"I'm so sorry," I said. "I didn't mean to upset you so much."

Nara pulled back, eyes red. "You did upset me." She wiped her face with the heel of her hand. "I still shouldn't have hit you."

"To be fair, I did deserve it," I said. "A little."

Nara showed a small smile, then she punched me in the arm.

"Whew," said Orrin, letting out his breath. "Now I'm hungry again."

"You just ate," said Vagid. He had been standing by the window the whole time, the way he did when he was working something out. "Someone who can flood a valley under a clear sky—if we fight him, we lose. So Evan goes first. That's the plan."

Nara looked at me for a long moment. "One chance," she said.

After breakfast, we were back on the coach. The ride was quiet but not tense. Orrin slept. Vagid read. Nara watched the road north, one hand resting on the knife at her belt. I spent the trip trying to figure out what I would say to Twelve. I only knew that he came from Earth, he had been to places that I recognized, his initials were J. N., and he liked spice. *Is it enough for me to connect with him?*

As the light was going, the coach stopped. We got out and unloaded our gear. Then the coach continued on its way.

We followed a cart track down through the pines. Vagid led. Nobody had to ask why.

The trees ended, and there it was.

Greywater Keep sat on a spit of rock out in the lake, and the lake was exactly as grey as advertised. It looked like the Blue Lagoon in Iceland, if someone had drained all the blue out. It had half a tower. Walls that had given up in places. A causeway ran out to it, or most of the way to it. It looked like someone had started building a castle, gotten bored halfway up the tower, and left it out in the rain for a few hundred years.

"Oh," said Vagid softly. "Look at the corbels on that—" He caught Nara's expression. "Never mind."

One window near the top of the tower was lit. Something small sat on the sill, the shape of a little building with a steep roof. Too far away to make out. Something about the roof bothered me, and I couldn't say why.

*His drawings and models.*

Nara looked at me. Her hand was on her knife. "One chance," she said.

I nodded and started across the causeway. Behind me, I heard the others follow.
