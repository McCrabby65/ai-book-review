# Chapter 32: Room for Improvement

Just before dinnertime, I went back to the Guild. I waited by the door for Prue to finish her day. A few minutes later, she came over. "You could get arrested for loitering like this."

"I would just tell them that I'm a lovestruck fool awaiting his lady," I said.

"Pft. Like that would work," said Prue. "So where are we going tonight?"

"I thought we would try the curry shop again. I haven't eaten there since we went the first time."

This time, Prue held out her hand, and I gladly took it. We walked like that all the way to the curry shop.

"Isn't that the same place that Mara and Vagid are going to dinner?" asked Prue.

"I set this train wreck in motion. I want to watch it happen."

"What's a tray-n?"

I thought about it for a few seconds.

"You know what, never mind. You'd hate it."

"I hate most things."

"Trains are loud, crowded, always late, and everyone on them is grumpy."

Prue considered this. "I think I'd like trains."

As we walked up to the curry shop, we saw Vagid and Mara already at a table. Before we could find our own, Mara spotted us. "Prue! Evan! Sit with us!"

I looked at Vagid, who was frozen bolt upright. I could see the souvenir in his hand under the table. He was holding it like it was highly explosive and might go off at any moment. His expression was one of fear tinged with joy. It was frightening.

He looked at me as we walked over. His eyes seemed to say, *Please don't*, so naturally I sat down immediately.

Prue ordered the same dish she had when we came here the first time, and I did the same. Mara ordered her curry extra hot and smiled sweetly at Vagid. I wasn't sure if Vagid was trying to be brave or just following whatever Mara did, but he ordered his curry extra hot too.

*Tonight's train wreck will be extra hot.*

As the server brought over our food, I asked them to bring a glass of milk for Vagid.

"Why would I drink milk with dinner?" asked Vagid. "I already have ale."

"Milk helps with the burning when you eat spicy foods," I said. Then I looked up at Vagid, who was glaring daggers at me. "Um. It's for me. I have a hard time with spicy foods."

Mara dug into her curry without so much as blinking. *Either Mara has no sense of pain, or she's been eating this since birth.*

Prue and I started on our curry. It was just as delicious as the first time. Vagid took a bite. His face didn't change. A moment later, without looking at me, he took my glass of milk and drank half of it.

"Is it good?" asked Mara.

"Mm," said Vagid. I could see sweat starting to form on his brow.

Prue looked at the half-empty glass, then at me. "I thought that was yours."

"I'm sharing," I said.

"Vagid is our party's cook. He's very good," I said.

"Ooh! You should cook for me!" said Mara.

*Wingman success—date two arranged. You're welcome.*

Vagid's ears went red. Under the table, something connected hard with my shin. I couldn't tell if it was Vagid or Prue. Possibly both.

The server came back by to see if we needed more milk.

"Where does the curry come from?" asked Vagid.

The server's face lit up. "You should ask the cook. He loves that question." A minute later, a round man in a stained apron came out of the kitchen, wiping his hands.

"My grandmother's recipe," he said proudly. "Well. Sort of. There was a foreigner who lived up at the old keep on the grey lake."

Across the table, Mara's smile stopped. Only for half a breath. Then it was back, as bright as ever.

*Did I imagine that?*

Before I could think about it, the cook went on. "Every month he'd send down a list of spices. Same ones, every time. Paid in silver and never set foot in town. My grandmother got curious about what he was making, so she asked the man who carried the orders up. Took her years to get it right."

I nearly dropped my spoon. *The keep on the grey water.*

"Isn't that the place you asked about earlier?" Mara asked Vagid.

Vagid's spoon stopped halfway to his mouth. "I like old keeps," he said. "It's a hobby."

"Ooh," said Mara. "That's so interesting!"

The cook beamed at him like he'd found a kindred spirit and launched into a story about a ruined tower near his cousin's farm. Vagid nodded through all of it. He'd have to keep liking old keeps forever now. Across the table, Prue had heard every word. So much for her being able to say she didn't know.

The cook was called back to the kitchen after a few minutes, something about checking the spiciness. Then Mara dropped a bomb on us. "So, Prue, you and Evan have been going out to dinner a lot. When are you going to get married?"

Without missing a beat, Prue said, "When Evan learns to haggle." Then she looked at me with a smirk.

I started coughing on the spoon of curry I had just put in my mouth. Vagid looked relieved not to be the center of attention.

"He still has a way to go," said Prue.

Vagid had gone quiet. He shifted in his seat twice, then thrust the souvenir across the table at Mara. "Here."

In his hand was a hairpin with a carving of a small fox.

"Vagid! It's beautiful!" said Mara, loud enough that the next table turned around. She put it in her strawberry-blonde hair right there. Vagid's ears went red enough to read by.

After regaining some of his composure, Vagid and I split the cost of the meal. I stood up and offered Prue my hand, which she took. Then Vagid slowly offered his hand to Mara. Mara looked at it for a split second, then grabbed it. "Eep," was all Vagid said.

As we left the curry shop, we said our goodbyes. Prue and I started toward her home, and Vagid and Mara went the other way. I looked back once to check on Vagid.&#32;

Vagid looked like a theme-park animatronic trying to walk and look casual at the same time. He was still holding Mara's hand, so that was good. *Does he think it will explode or something?*

Our walk to Prue's house was quiet. As we passed the Guild, her hand tightened in mine. Neither of us said anything about the soldiers coming in the morning.

We got to her house, and I pulled her into a hug. I took my time with the kiss. If I was going to be graded on my performance, I wanted top marks.

"That was better," said Prue.

"Still room for improvement?"

"There's always room for improvement," said Prue. "But you're improving."

After a second's pause, she said, "Come by the Guild in the morning. Late. After they've gone."

I nodded. "Good night, Prue."

"Good night, Evan."
