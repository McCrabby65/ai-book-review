# Chapter 30: Dead Weight

The coach stopped at dusk, as it always did. We ate in the common room and went to bed early. The next day, the coach dropped us off in Sedgewick. We went to the house written on the job slip and talked to the requester.

On our way to the last place they saw the goblins, Vagid gave us our orders.

"Orrin, when we get there, find a safe place to shoot from. Nara, keep the wind blowing toward us to hide our scent. Evan and I will go into the village," said Vagid.

We reached the goblin village and Orrin disappeared up a tree. He looked happier than I had seen him in a week. Nara said quietly, "Gentle Breeze," and a light breeze started blowing toward us from the goblin village. Vagid and I drew our swords, and Vagid walked straight into their camp like he'd been invited.

I tried to remember everything Stella had taught me, which turned out to be one lesson: how to land. A goblin swung at my head. I fell backward, exactly as I had been taught, and his club passed over where my head had been.

"Nice dodge," said Orrin from the tree.

"I meant to do that," I said, lying on my back.

By the time I got up, it was over. We collected the left ears from the goblins. Instead of heading back to report the job finished, we went to the hill above Lower Lumenhold, to the cave where we fought the kobolds.

When we got there, Vagid had Nara and Orrin check the surroundings for any people.&#32;

"Search," said Nara. After a few seconds, she said, "Nothing and no one near."

"How does it work?" I asked. "Your search magic."

"The wind carries back heartbeats," said Nara. "If there's a heartbeat, I find it."

Orrin came back and reported the same thing. Nothing. No soldiers. No guards.

The cave was still open, which seemed strange, but I couldn't figure out why. *It's probably nothing.*

We entered the cave and headed to our previous base camp. Once we arrived, we set up our bedrolls, then went to the storeroom. We checked that the crates were still how we had left them, then moved them aside.

As we were getting ready to enter the storeroom, I saw Orrin look back toward our camp.

"Is something wrong?" I asked.

"I just felt like I was being watched," said Orrin.

*We're going in.*

*Good luck, Evan,* said a tired voice.

Vagid had Nara cast Search and had Orrin check the other side of the storeroom door. Orrin came back and shook his head. We entered the dungeon and proceeded to the oubliette. Vagid opened the door and we all filed through the opening. There was a quiet click as the door closed behind Vagid.

*Tired Guy?* No reply. I was cut off from him again.

We went up the steps. This time I didn't trip or stumble and we made it all the way to the archive door. Vagid signaled Nara to search. Nara signaled all clear. Vagid had Orrin open the door and we entered the archive for the second time.

Vagid signaled Orrin to wait by our exit and Nara to keep listening with Search, while he and I took the shelves by the door.&#32;

Vagid and I went through all the documents on the wall we missed the first time. There were several documents in English. Most of them had drawings of buildings from my world. All of them were signed by J. N. *Wow, they are an amazing artist.*

Some of the drawings looked very familiar, like I had seen something like them before, but I couldn't place where. The drawings were titled, but I couldn't read the name. It wasn't English, but it was definitely not Hallish either. Below the initials of the artist were numbers. One of the drawings had "3" in Hallish, and another had "4".

Vagid was reading a document, and glanced over to see the drawings. After a moment, he handed me the document and pointed to the text in Hallish.

> Transfer of Items Three and Four (Foreign Models, Subject Twelve) from Lumenhold. By order of the Chancellor, on the Foreigner's complaint, they are moved from the archive to the keep on the grey water, where the rest of his work is held. The key is with the one who minds the keep.

Subject Twelve? The summon right before me. *Are they still here in this world?*

Three and Four, just like the drawings. The models were gone, but the plans were still here. I put the document in my pack.

Nara turned toward Orrin and said, "...a heartbeat." It had appeared out of nowhere, a heartbeat where there had been nothing a moment before.

Orrin started to turn, then a hand appeared from behind him and covered his mouth. He stiffened and sucked in a breath. "Ghrf," he tried to say.

Orrin fell into the room, a nasty bloodstain on the back of his armor. My stomach dropped. There was a person in all black behind him.

*They look like a ninja. Head to toe black. Only their eyes showing. *Okay, Ninja Dude.**

I watched Ninja Dude look at Vagid, then at me. *He hasn't figured out which of us is important.*

Then Ninja Dude looked at Nara and seemed to make a decision. He slowly moved into the room toward Nara. I moved between Ninja Dude and Nara. He stopped advancing. "Ruiner," said Ninja Dude in Hallish.

"What does that mean?" asked Vagid. "Is that a person or place?"

I could see that was the wrong thing to say. Vagid had just told him he wasn't the one he wanted, and that made Vagid expendable. Ninja Dude moved toward Vagid. He took one step and hissed. I looked down and saw that Orrin had taken one of his arrows and stabbed Ninja Dude's foot with it. Ninja Dude turned to finish Orrin, then went rigid. Vagid had moved faster than I had ever seen him move. He had driven his sword through Ninja Dude's back. He pulled the sword free with a quick twist, the same motion he'd used on the berry-patch chimera. Ninja Dude folded to the floor without a sound. Vagid stared at the blade like it belonged to someone else.

Nara started looking at Orrin's back. "It's not a deep cut, but it is bleeding a lot," she said. "Since that was an assassin, the blade was probably coated in poison."

"Vagid, what do we do?" I asked.

Vagid was still looking at his sword, lost in thought.

"Nara, can you heal him?" I asked.

"No. He needs a real healer, and we can't take him to Lumenhold. They'd know what a poisoned wound means," she said.

"Is there anything we can do to slow down the poison?"

"Not unless we know what the poison is," she said.

*Crap!*

I looked over at Vagid. He was still gazing blankly at his sword. It must have been the first time he'd had to kill a person.

I stepped over to Vagid and said, "I'm sorry." Then I slapped him hard across the face.

I could see his face register the pain, and anger flashed across it. His sword was at my throat in an instant. "Explain."

"Orrin is wounded, probably poisoned. Nara and I don't know what to do. We need you, Vagid. Orrin needs you," I said.&#32;

Slowly Vagid lowered his sword. "Did you check for the antidote?" he asked.

"Antidote?" asked Nara.

"An assassin with a poisoned weapon would have an antidote on them in case they poisoned the wrong person," he said as he knelt beside the assassin's body. I knelt down too and we both searched the body. We found a lot of weapons which we set aside. Vagid found a small vial of dark liquid, and paused.

"Well, is that it?" I asked.

"Probably," he said.

"Then give it to Orrin," I said.

"How much is the right amount?" he asked. "This could be a single dose or a dozen doses. We don't know which it is, and it could be dangerous too. We need to be cautious. I'll dilute a drop in a mug of water. Then we give that to Orrin and see how he does. But we also need to figure out how to get all of us out of here."

While Vagid mixed up the antidote, I tried to figure out how to get us out of here. I was thinking, but not getting anywhere.

We needed a way to move Orrin. The stairs were too narrow for two people to carry him between them, and too long and twisting for one person to do it quickly. And Nara only had magic. *Wait!*

Nara had magic.

*This is a really crazy idea.*

"Nara, you can make a barrier with wind, right?" I asked.

"Yes, but I don't see how that's going to help us here," she said.

"Can you shape the barrier?" I asked.

"To a degree. Why?"

"We want to get Orrin and us out of here, but the way down the stairs is long, narrow, with twists and turns," I said.

"Evan, I know that. Get to the point," she said.

"Could you put a barrier down the stairs, like a trough of air? We can slide down with Orrin behind us. Then when we get to the end, you could use wind magic to slow us down."

Nara stared at me for a moment, then sighed. "That's an interesting idea, but it would drain me before we got a dozen steps down. A flat barrier is easy; a trough that bends around corners in a stairway that narrow isn't."

"I can think of one way to get us at least onto the stairs," said Vagid as he gave Orrin the diluted antidote. "It's the same way we got Evan back to Halden's."

"This is going to suck, isn't it?" I asked.

"It could always be worse," said Nara. "Orrin could be naked."

*Wow. That one's never going away.*

"You can't carry the packs and Orrin, and I need my sword hand," said Vagid. "Give me a few minutes and I'll take the packs down a ways and leave them, then when we catch up, I can carry them down further while you rest."

Vagid carried our packs two at a time down the stairs. He came back after a few moments, then tied Orrin to my back. Once Orrin was secure, Vagid entered the stairway. I followed with Orrin on my back. Last came Nara. She closed the stairway door behind us.

We took our time going down the stairs. We would go around ten to fifteen steps down, Vagid would move the bags, and I would take a break by leaning against the wall. Somewhere around the fortieth step, Orrin mumbled, "Not the face," into my ear. At least he was still in there. After hours of this, we made it down to the oubliette door. Nara and Vagid helped me by untying the ropes holding Orrin on. Vagid opened the oubliette door and took our packs through. Getting Orrin through the oubliette was going to be a problem.

Once Orrin was untied from my back, we sat him down on the stairs leaning against the wall. We also had to get a good look at the wound, so the armor around his chest and back would need to come off.

Nara and I were able to get his armor off and handed to Vagid, who took it out into the corridor. Then we got a good look at the wound. The skin around the cut was red, swollen and warm. The rest of Orrin was pale and his skin was clammy. I put my ear to his chest and his heartbeat sounded steady. At least it wasn't racing.

"We'll need to pull him through the door," said Vagid. "Evan, you come on through. Then, Nara, can you lay Orrin down on his chest? Evan and I can get his arms and pull him through."

"I can do that," said Nara.

I crawled through the door, then Nara slowly lowered Orrin face first to the floor. Vagid reached through the door and pulled Orrin's arms through, and I took his right arm. With some light cursing, we finally got Orrin through the oubliette door. Vagid and I were standing there panting. Nara crawled through the door and joined us. We were all filthy from the years of dust that was on the stairs and the grime that was on the dungeon floor.

*I really hope Orrin didn't have his mouth open when we pulled him through the door.*

I sat with Orrin while Vagid and Nara took our packs to camp. When they returned, we tied Orrin to my back again and they helped me walk him back to camp.

We laid Orrin out on his bedding face down. I started a fire while Vagid and Nara cleaned up Orrin and dressed his cut.

Vagid made a stew from the jerky and cheese we had brought. It was creamy and delicious. *At least this cheese was paid for.* We sat there chatting about what had happened and what it might mean for Orrin.

"How long before we know?" I asked. "If the antidote worked, I mean."

"If it was the antidote, we should know something by morning," said Vagid. "If it was not an antidote or was only part of one..." He didn't finish.

"What are his chances?" asked Nara.

"I just don't know. There are too many things that could go wrong," said Vagid. "He could be fine. He could have a permanent brain fog. He could be unable to use his bow. He could still die."

A weak voice came from Orrin's bedding: "I'm not dead yet."

"Well, give it time," said Vagid, laughing.

"You had us worried, Orrin," said Nara, wiping tears away. "Evan, you made the fire too smoky."

*It's a perfectly good fire.*

"I carried you on my back down the stairs, so we're even now," I said.

"Why can't I move?" asked Orrin. His voice was weak, and it seemed to tire him out. "And unless I was naked when you carried me, we're still not even."

Orrin was asleep before any of us could answer.

We set up the watch schedule. I was first, then Nara, then Vagid. Nara went to sleep. But Vagid sat by the fire with a rag, wiping his sword. He'd cleaned it three times since dinner. It had been clean after the first.

"Are you okay?" I asked. "If you're still mad that I hit you, I'm really sorry about it."

"No. That needed to happen," said Vagid.

"Is it Ninja Dude?" I asked.

"What's a Nen—jzah dood?" asked Vagid.

"It's what my home would call the assassin," I said.

"I don't even remember stabbing him. I saw him start to attack Orrin again and suddenly I was standing there with my sword covered in the assassin's blood and he was dead at my feet," he said.

"It was reflexive. You wanted to protect your friends," I said.

"Why? Why don't I feel anything about killing another person?" he asked.

*It's shell shock, but without the shell.*

"We call it shock. Your brain is trying to keep you safe and get you through something very difficult. So it puts the feelings on hold until it's safe to have them," I said.&#32;

I put my arm around his shoulder and said, "Just because you don't feel anything now doesn't mean you won't ever. It may be hours, days, months, or years later, but it will come."

"Hm," said Vagid.

"And when it does, we will be here for you," I said. "Like good friends should be."

Vagid nodded, then went to bed.

I kept watch and thought about what we had learned. Orrin might be out of action for a while, but we had found a clue about the person summoned before me. We had also left a dead assassin and a trail of footprints behind us. Whoever had sent him would soon wonder why he hadn't come back.

*The keep on the grey water. Somewhere out there, Subject Twelve's work was waiting. Maybe Subject Twelve was too.*
