# Lumenhold — Units

Garrick Ashford stared at the papers on his desk. The stack was a finger-width out of place from where he had left it. *Silas.* Garrick reached out to straighten the papers, but stopped himself. The clay horse his daughter made for him was still off, which was exactly how he had left it. There was a knock at the door.

"Enter," he said.

A soldier entered the office. "Sir! The Lord Chancellor requests your presence."

"Very well. Tell him I am on my way."

"Sir! Yes, sir!" said the soldier before leaving.

Garrick stood and pulled his sleeve down over the bandage on his forearm. The cut was healing. The man who'd given it to him wasn't.

Garrick buckled on his sword and swung his cloak over his shoulders. He left to give his report.

When he arrived at the Chancellor's office, he was escorted inside and told to wait. Garrick could hear Silas in his quarters talking quietly to someone. *Probably Wren.*

"My dear, you are too kind-hearted. You must learn to treat servants like pebbles on the road. If they get in your way, you kick them out of your way," said Silas gently.

"But, Papa, I was just playing with Mica. We were having fun," Garrick heard Wren say.

"Mica is the maid's daughter, Wren. She is beneath you."

"Yes, Papa," said Wren sadly.

"Go. Your tutor should be ready for your lessons," said Silas.

After a moment, Silas Malgrave entered. "Report!"

"Garnerby is flooded. There is no sign of the Ruiner," said Garrick.

"And my man?" asked Silas.

"Monsters," said Garrick.

Silas's eyes narrowed. He didn't like the answer. He couldn't refute it.

"The ritual will happen before the first snow," said Silas.

"All will be ready, my lord."

"And Twelve is no longer needed. I'm sending Harna to deal with him," said Silas. "She should be able to handle him."

Harna. Garrick had heard of her but never met her. The Widow Bride. She had been the best assassin on the market for several years. *Ever since Stroya was mysteriously killed.*

Garrick's jaw tightened.

"Get out of here and do your job," said Silas.

"My lord," Garrick said, and left the Chancellor. He went to the dungeons to double-check that all of the units were there.

At the guard station, he saw a young soldier he didn't know. "You. What is your name?" asked Garrick.

"Sir! Dorran, sir!"

"Dorran, how many units are currently in the dungeon?" asked Garrick.

"There are four hundred and thirty-five, sir!" answered Dorran.

"And the remaining shipments?" pressed Garrick.

"They should arrive by the end of the month, sir."

*It should be well before the first snow.*

As Garrick turned to go, a soldier dragged a girl no more than eight summers old, her dress torn and dirty, toward the cells. The girl was someone Garrick had seen playing with Wren outside the Chancellor's office a few times. *Mica.*

The girl wasn't crying. She'd stopped, the way children do when crying hasn't helped. Somewhere behind his eyes, Silas's voice, gentle: *If they get in your way, you kick them out of your way.* Garrick's hand found the bandage under his sleeve and pressed it.

"Good," Garrick said, because Dorran was waiting for an answer. Then he walked back to his office. As he entered, he checked the items on his desk. The papers were still a finger-width out of place, as he had left them. The clay horse was still crooked.

There was a knock at the door.

"Enter," he said.

A soldier set down a sheet of paper: the date of the ritual. Garrick read it once and squared it to the edge of the desk. *Not again.*
