# Chapter 26: Harmless

In the morning, we told Tobin there were no more kobolds. He wished us safe travels, and we walked to Lumenhold to catch the noon coach to Millbrook. We had time for breakfast first. We ate in silence, but we heard gossip about trouble in the castle.

A man at the next table said, "I heard somebody broke into the castle!"

We all held our breath, waiting to hear what was stolen.

Then a woman at a table farther away said, "Yeah, they stole the Chancellor's cheese. And they got his ham too."

*I had never been so glad to be blamed for cheese.*

Vagid had stopped eating until he heard that it was the cheese. Then he started chewing again. Orrin had started to take a drink of water. When she said "cheese," he started coughing and choking on it.

After a coughing fit, Orrin said, "Wrong pipe."

Nara just sipped her tea. She hadn't touched her food.

I suddenly wasn't very hungry either. I just drank my tea and stared into my mug.

At noon, we loaded our gear onto the coach and boarded. We rode in silence, since there were other passengers in the coach. *Quick way to get caught: "Oh, look at the notes we stole from the castle."*

The notes could wait until we were close to the river and there was enough noise to keep anyone from hearing what we were talking about.

The rest of the coach ride was uneventful. We arrived back in Millbrook the next day at dinnertime.

It was starting to get dark, so we decided to go over the remaining notes in the morning, after we met for breakfast.

The thought of the notes just sitting there weighed on my mind. I tossed and turned for a long time and almost got up and just read them, but I was finally able to get to sleep.

The next morning, we met for breakfast. The others looked like I felt. Apparently everyone had a restless night.

We each ate quickly, then headed to the river, where Nara had carved the boulder.

I separated the notes into three piles and gave one each to Nara and Vagid. I kept the last set.

"Orrin, keep watch in case anyone comes by," said Vagid.

We stared at each other for a few seconds. None of us wanted to be the first to read. Then Vagid started reading his notes.

> *Core Trial One*
>
> *Proposal: substitute monster cores for units. Cost of four hundred fifty units at eleven rays each is prohibitive. Equivalent value in cores is obtainable through the Guild at a fraction of the price.*
>
> *Cores used: one hundred twelve, various (goblin, wolf, cave bear).*
>
> *Result: subject arrived. Subject did not speak. Subject's form was altered. Fur on the arms and back. Teeth unsuited to its mouth.*
>
> *Subject restrained. Subject attacked the restraints, then itself. Expired on the fourth day.*
>
> *Conclusion: cores are a viable substitute. Alterations likely due to low-quality cores. Recommend higher grade for the next trial.*

"Money. They did it to save money," said Vagid after a few seconds.

I was feeling sick to my stomach. *Maybe breakfast wasn't such a good idea.*

I went next.

> *Core Trial Two*
>
> *Proposal: higher quality monster cores will prevent the alterations. Due to higher quality, fewer cores were available.*
>
> *Cores used: fifty-three, higher grade (troll, wyvern, basilisk)*
>
> *Result: subject arrived. Subject did not speak. Alterations more severe than Trial One. Horns. Additional limbs. Markings on the skin.*
>
> *Subject broke its restraints on the second day. Two handlers killed. Subject escaped through the lower drain toward the river.*
>
> *Search abandoned after nine days. Subject presumed dead.*
>
> *Conclusion: alterations increase with core strength. Recommend combining high- and low-grade cores for the next trial.*

"Presumed dead," I said. *Clearly that's wrong. We killed it.*

I stared at the notes, not knowing what to say. Then I heard Nara start reading her set.

> *Core Trial Three*
>
> *Proposal: combining high- and low-grade cores should stabilize the alterations and keep the subject pliable.*
>
> *Cores used: thirty-seven high-grade (minotaur, basilisk, wyvern) and thirty-five low-grade (goblin, orc, kobold, slime).*
>
> *Result: no direct observers or handlers found at site**. Presumed** killed. No notes on the appearance of the subject. No notes on the abilities of the subject.*
>
> *No remains of observers or handlers were found at the site. Large splatters of blood were found on almost every surface. A large bloody streak led out of the site, then disappeared into the woods.*
>
> *Conclusion: at this time, further experimentation is not warranted due to the danger.*
>
> *Addendum: a hand with a ring was found several leagues** away. The ring was identified as belonging to one of the observers.*

Nara looked like she was about to throw up. She folded the notes and handed them back to me. I could see tears in her eyes. Orrin didn't say anything. He just kept scanning the tree line, as if a chimera would jump out at any moment. Vagid stared at his notes with his hand to his forehead.

"Is it still out there?" asked Vagid.

"Probably," I said. *I really hope I'm wrong, though.*

I sat on the stone bench and closed my eyes.

*Tired Guy?* I asked.

*Yes, Evan,* said Tired Guy.

*The chimera that we killed in the berry patch, you were able to talk with him, right?* I asked.

*Yes,* said Tired Guy.

*And the chimera who was part of Trial One, were you able to talk to them?* I asked.

*Yes,* said Tired Guy.

*Do you know if you have talked to the one who was part of the third trial?* I asked.

*No. I haven't talked with the summoned from the third trial,* said Tired Guy. *That could mean that they are still alive, or that they died in a desecrated location.*

"Crap," I said. "Tired Guy hasn't met its soul. Either it died somewhere he can't see, like the church or Lumenhold, or it *is* still out there."

We sat there for a moment, wondering whether that chimera was still out in the world, and if so, where.

"Let's get these notes in with the others at the Guild," I said.

The others nodded, and we set off for the Guild. We joined Prue's line and waited our turn.

When we reached the head of the line, Prue said, "Back from your trip? I don't suppose you caught a cheese thief?"

"Um... no," I said. "But right now we need to get Cuthbert to add some pages to our file."

Prue looked at me with one eyebrow raised. She clearly wanted to ask about my trip, but I couldn't bring her into it. It was too dangerous.

"Weird, but whatever," said Prue. Then she led us up to Cuthbert's office.

As usual, Prue opened the Guild master's door without knocking and said, "Cutty! Plan B is here to see you!"

Cuthbert was writing something down in a ledger and didn't immediately respond. Once he finished, he said, "To what do I owe this visit?"

"We have more notes to add to our Guild file," said Vagid. He nodded to me. I carried the notes over to Cuthbert's desk and set them down next to the ledger. Cuthbert got up, retrieved our file from his cabinets, brought it back, and opened it on top of the ledger.

When the file opened, I noticed the top page was from Rachel's notes. One line stood out:

> *Bought a cellar slime from the farmer in Barley Ford. Harmless. It'll keep the curious away.*

When I had read that line before, it didn't mean anything to me. But now that I knew how Rachel died, I understood. Her death had twisted the slime into something else. The slime killing all those people had been an accident. Rachel had never meant for it to kill anyone.

I'd thought Rachel was a killer. I'd even wondered if I'd become like her. I was wrong. About both of us, maybe.

The sound of Cuthbert's stamp brought me out of my thoughts. He had stamped the new notes and put them into the file without reading them. Just like last time.

"If that is all, please see yourselves out," said Cuthbert.

We left his office and headed out of the Guild. It was nearly lunchtime, so we decided to walk back to the cafe. On the way, I told the group what I'd read in Cuthbert's office about Rachel and the slime.

"A cellar slime," said Vagid. "I said it myself. Cellar slimes don't even sting."

"Evan, you look relieved. Was this bothering you?" asked Nara.

"Yeah. I had thought that if Rachel, a healer, could turn into a murderer, then maybe I could too," I said.

"No way!" said Orrin. "You don't have any magic."

"You can kill without magic," I said. "We do it to monsters all the time."

"Evan, killing a monster and killing a human are completely different things," said Vagid.

"Yes, and besides, if you had asked me, I would have told you that you would never be a killer," said Nara. "None of us would. We don't have the heart for it."

The chimera in the berry patch had been a person once. I didn't say it. I didn't need to. Vagid's face said he'd realized it too.

When we got to the cafe, it was a bit crowded, so we had to wait for a table. When a table opened up, we sat down. I ordered an boar steak with vegetables and tea. Vagid ordered orc short ribs with mead.

*Apparently orc is a normal thing to eat here. I'm sticking with the boar.*

Nara ordered what looked like a Caesar salad with tea. Then it was Orrin's turn to order.

"Two orc steaks, hold the vegetables, with a large ale," said Orrin.

Nara turned to stare at Orrin.

"What? I skipped breakfast," said Orrin.

"Your second breakfast, maybe. You definitely had the first one with us this morning," said Vagid.

"I'm still growing," said Orrin.

"Out, not up," I said.

Orrin faked a hurt look before laughing with the rest of us. It was the first time we'd laughed all day.

When we finished our lunch, we each went our own way, agreeing to meet up in the morning for breakfast.

I walked back to the Guild. I wanted to ask Prue out to dinner again. I couldn't tell her about the notes, but I still wanted to spend time with her, even if it didn't go anywhere.

When I got to the Guild, I joined Prue's line and waited my turn.

"Twice in one day. People will talk," said Prue when I reached the front.

"Is this where you tell me about the trip?" asked Prue.

"This is where I ask you to dinner," I said. "Tomorrow night, if you're available."

Prue looked at me for a moment, then took a blank job slip, wrote something on it, and slid it across the counter to me.

I took the slip and stepped away from the counter to read it.

> I'll have to move some things around.
>
> - Rebraid hair: move to tonight.
> - Bathe pet orc: move to day after tomorrow.
> - Help the orphans: postpone to next week.
>
> ...
>
> Done. —P

After reading the slip, I turned to see Prue watching me. I waved to her with a smile on my face. She kept her expression bored, but winked at me.

I started back to the chapel, but changed my mind and headed to the river, where our boulder carving was. I stretched out on the grass by the river, one knee up and my other foot propped across it, and listened to the water.

*Tired Guy, are you there?* I asked.

*I'm here, Evan,* said Tired Guy.

*I found Rachel's note about the cellar slime,* I said. *She never meant to kill anyone.*

*No. She didn't, *said Tired Guy.

*You knew that I was worried about becoming a killer. Like I thought Rachel was. Like I thought Xavier was, back in the church**,* I said.

*Yes. I did,* said Tired Guy. *Some things only mean something if you find them yourself.*

*I suppose so,* I said. *I probably wouldn't have believed it if you had just told me.*

*Did you know how long the berry patch man had been a chimera?* I asked.

*Yes,* said Tired Guy.

*Why didn't you tell me?* I asked. *Or was this another thing I needed to find myself?*

*You already know the answer, don't you?* asked Tired Guy.

*I guess I do,* I said.

*How have you been doing otherwise**, Evan?* asked Tired Guy.

*I asked Prue out to dinner again. I don't want to involve her, but it makes me happy when we're together,* I said. *It's very confusing.*

*It often is,* said Tired Guy.

*What does that mean?* I asked. *Tired Guy?*

Apparently even gods don't want to talk about dating.
