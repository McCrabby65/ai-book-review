# Chapter 24: Oubliette

On the coach trip back to Lumenhold, I decided to bring up Mara, mostly for Vagid's benefit.

"Nara, what are your thoughts on anyone dating Mara?" I asked. "I get the impression she doesn't pick up on subtle hints."

"Mara is a sweet girl, but subtlety would just fly right past her," said Nara. "With Mara, you would need to be explicit and direct."

"So something like 'Mara, do you want to go to dinner with me?' might just sound like two people eating dinner, with nothing romantic about it?" I asked.

"Exactly," said Nara. "You would need to make it clear that you meant it romantically."

"So maybe 'Mara, I would like you to have a romantic dinner with me' would be clear enough?" I asked.

"That would be better," said Nara. "But even that might not be enough."

"Wow. So you'd need to tell her you wanted it to be romantic, with the chance of becoming more, without sounding creepy," I said. "That's a tough puzzle."

I glanced at Vagid out of the corner of my eye. His face was red, but he was definitely making mental notes.

"Just walk up to her, tell her you're in love with her, and kiss her," said Orrin, way too loudly. "How hard can it be?"

*That explains a lot about Orrin.*

"Nara, how many times can you cast Search in a day?" asked Vagid.

"I've never really tried to find out, but I would guess at least a dozen times, if not more. It doesn't take that much energy," said Nara.

"I was thinking we would need to spend a few days in the dungeon to figure out which tower has guards at the base," said Vagid. "But there's a chance none of the towers will have guards posted."

"No guards. Hm," I said. "That would be smarter. Then the only way to know would be to see which one is entered the least, or which one the Chancellor goes into the most."

"Right. And that could take some time. At least a few days, possibly more," said Vagid.

"I won't be able to tell who is a guard and who is the Chancellor using Search. All Search tells me is whether a person or monster is there," said Nara.

"I suppose it would be too much to hope for the Chancellor to be a real monster," I said.

"Orrin, you may need to scout ahead to see who Nara is sensing with Search. Is that okay?" asked Vagid.

"Yeah, it shouldn't be a problem," said Orrin.

"I think our base should be the intersection just before the branch that leads to the storeroom. From there, we can still get away, and if we're found, we can say we were making sure no kobolds were left," said Vagid.

"Couldn't we just stay at Tobin's workshop?" asked Orrin.

"No. Going back and forth to the cave for several days would look suspicious to the villagers. And if a guard heard about it while picking up his boots, the guards would go after Tobin and his family," said Vagid.

That night, we stayed at the same inn as on our last trip to Lumenhold. Dinner was slices of ham with bread and cheese. *I never thought I'd be eating a ham and cheese sandwich in an isekai. At least it's not rabbit.*

I spotted the carter from our last trip at a nearby table, complaining to the man next to him. "They had me luggin' crate after crate of parchment, ink, and lamp oil. Then they wanted it put in the bottom of that tower no one goes into. Not put away, mind you. Just stacked inside the door."

*That makes finding the tower easier. We just need the one nobody uses.*

At another table, two clerks on their way back to the castle were talking.

"They want the documents first thing in the morning for the Chancellor to sign," said one. "I can't get them done in time."

"Don't worry," said the other. "The Chancellor's out inspecting something at Thornspire. He won't be back till next week."

Toward the end of dinner, there was a commotion out front. When one of the barmaids came by, I asked, "Is something happening outside?"

"Nah. That's just a bunch of teamsters heading to Thornspire. They been coming through a lot lately. Never stop for anything but water and feed for the horses. Then off they go again," she said.

The barmaid left to attend to other tables, and I looked over at Vagid. He nodded.

*Four hundred units**. That's why the teamsters don't stop for long.*

We went to our room in silence.

The coach left early the next morning, and we arrived at Lumenhold just before dinner. We walked the short way around to the Lower, and Vagid knocked on Tobin's door.

After a second, the door opened, and Tobin said, "Plan B! You're back already?"

"We're on a job to check for other caves and kobold dens in the area, and we thought we'd stop in and say hi," said Vagid.

"Um... That's great... but... I... I can't pay you anymore," said Tobin meekly.

"Don't worry, the Guild is covering this," said Nara, smiling.

"Oh. Well, that's really good news," said Tobin.

Tobin's wife came out with Ronar following. "I'll start a fire, and we can all sit and chat," she said.

"You remember my wife, Koren, and my son, Ronar," said Tobin.

"Of course," said Nara. "So good to see you again."

Koren went to the firepit in front of the house and lit the fire while Nara kept watch over Ronar.

"Have you seen any more kobolds in the area?" asked Vagid. "Or heard about any?"

"No, no mention of kobolds. The guards usually talk about one thing or another, but they been tight-lipped lately," said Tobin. "It quieted down a bunch after the Chancellor's fancy coach left the other day."

"Well, we'll be camping in the cave while we're here. We plan to search the other branches of the kobolds' cave, then move on to other caves nearby," I said. "If you hear anything about kobolds or other monsters, let us know."

We chatted about other things, then said our goodbyes.

We lit our lanterns and set out for the cave. Orrin led the way in, followed by Nara, me, and Vagid last. Nara cast Search at each intersection to be sure the cave was still clear. We paused for a moment at the spot where we had found Sava. No one needed to say anything, but we all remembered. When we reached the intersection just before the branch to the storeroom, we set up camp.

"I really hated lying to Tobin and his family," said Nara. "I know it's for their protection, but it's still a lie."

"Yeah," said Orrin. "It sucks."

"If something goes wrong, that is the only thing we can do to protect them," said Vagid. "Not much, but it's something."

Still, lying to a friend made us all feel dirty. We took our usual watches and tried to sleep.

In the morning, we set out for the storeroom.

"Today we just learn the layout," said Vagid quietly. "Nobody goes past the second level."

We found the crates still blocking the hole in the wall. We gently pushed them forward on the hides, making sure not to topple them.

Once the crates were far enough in, Orrin slipped around them and started moving them to a side wall. When he was done, the path to the storeroom door was open. We all turned toward Vagid.

Vagid pointed to Nara, then made a circle in the air with his finger. *Check the area with Search.*

"Search," said Nara softly. After a few seconds, she shook her head. *Nothing near.*

Vagid pointed to Orrin, then to the door. *Scout ahead.*

Orrin nodded and silently slipped out of the storeroom. After a minute, he came back, looked at Vagid, and nodded. *Area clear.*

We left the storeroom and gathered in the hallway. Vagid pointed to Orrin, then gestured forward.

Orrin nodded, then quietly started walking. We took our places: Nara, me, then Vagid. Orrin led us down a long hall lined with doors. Each door had a small opening at head height, really just a horizontal slit. I doubted I could have fit more than four fingers into it.

At the end of the hallway was a staircase leading up. Orrin started up the steps, and we followed as quietly as we could. When we reached the next level, Orrin checked the immediate area, then we all stepped out. Vagid held up his fist. *Wait.*

I noticed something odd: the stairs only went from this level to the one below. *Where are the stairs up?*

We gathered around Vagid, who drew the same circle in the air for Nara.

"Search," said Nara, barely above a breath. "This level is clear. The one above us has a few people in cells near the stairs."

Vagid nodded.

"The level above that has a lot of people," said Nara.

Vagid sent Orrin down the hall with a nod. Orrin disappeared into the dark.

After a few minutes, Orrin returned and gave a thumbs-up, a gesture he'd picked up from me, then pointed to his eyes. *All clear, but there's something we need to see?*

We followed Orrin down the hall to a small bricked-over opening. Vagid brought his lantern closer, then said under his breath, "An oubliette."

I had never heard that word before, so I signaled that I didn't know what it was. Vagid leaned close. "It's where you put people you want to forget about. Then you seal it up."

Vagid knelt down, pointed at a stone, then pointed at me. I looked closer. Scratched into it, in English, was "RL →." Rachel had been here. One hundred and seventy-five years ago, another Otherborn had stood right where I was standing.

I looked at Vagid. "RL. Rachel Lin," I said, almost soundlessly.

Vagid nodded, and Orrin led us on to the far end of the hall, where the stairs were. Every set of stairs so far had been at the opposite end from the last.

"The first chancellor built it this way," said Vagid quietly. "Every prisoner had to be walked past every other prisoner on the way down."

*Someone sat down and planned that.*

We started up, but instead of another level, we reached a bricked-up wall at the top.

Vagid and Orrin went down to the landing to talk quietly about what to do. While I waited, I leaned against the brick wall at the top of the stairs. One of the stones shifted under my weight with a scraping sound. Nara grabbed my arm and slowly pulled me away from the wall. Orrin and Vagid were staring up at us.

"Is someone there?" came a muffled voice.

Vagid held up his fist, then pointed down the stairs. We crept back down and along the hallway, stopping by the oubliette.

"Sorry. I didn't expect the wall to be so loose," I said, keeping my voice low.

"It would make too much noise to break through that wall," said Vagid. "We need a different path."

I looked back at the stairs, then remembered we were standing right next to the oubliette. "RL," I said.

"Rachel left a message, and there was an arrow in it," I said. "What does it point to?"

Vagid held the lantern up to the message, near the bottom of the bricked-over doorway. "Which direction is it pointing?"

"To the right," I said.

Vagid started pressing the stones to the right of the message. When he pressed the third one, it slid in with a soft click.

We looked at each other. Then Orrin pushed against the bricked-over door. It swung inward, along with part of the wall beside it.

Now there was an opening in the wall, about two feet square. It would be a tight fit for Orrin and me, but Nara and Vagid wouldn't have any problems.

*So much for just learning the layout.*

Vagid took off his pack, knelt, and held his lantern into the opening. Steps led upward, covered in a thick layer of dust. He drew the circle in the air for Nara.

"Search," said Nara. After a second, she added, "Clear."

Vagid looked at Orrin, then at the hole. Orrin rolled his eyes, but he took off his pack and crawled in first. He always did.

One by one, we squeezed through, pushing our packs ahead of us. The stairway beyond was just wide enough for one person, so each of us had to climb a few steps to make room for the next. Vagid came last, on his hands and knees, much faster than the rest of us.

Once Vagid was through, he pushed the door closed to see how the mechanism worked. It shut with the same soft click, and for a moment nobody breathed. Then Vagid found a worn iron ring set into the stone beside it and pulled. The door eased open again. He closed it and gave us a nod. *We can get back out.*

"Were there any prints in the dust?" asked Vagid softly.

"None," said Orrin.

So this stairway hadn't been used in a long time. Probably not since Rachel.
