# K2: The Ruiner

Sitting at his desk, General Garrick Ashford was staring out the window, contemplating how much longer he would be allowed to remain in his current position, when a knock came at his door.

"Enter," said Garrick.

The door opened and a soldier stepped in.

"Sir!" said the soldier, snapping to attention.

"Report," said Garrick.

"The Chancellor demands your immediate presence at Lumenhold, General Ashford."

*Demands? That's new.*

"Very well," said Garrick.

Garrick got up from his desk, buckled on his sword, fastened his cloak, and set out for Lumenhold.

On the way, he turned the words over. Demands. Immediate.

When he arrived, Garrick learned that the Chancellor had cut his inspection at Thornspire short. He was led to the Chancellor's office. As he neared it, he could hear the Chancellor screaming at someone, but he couldn't make out the words.

The soldier who had escorted Garrick knocked on the door.

"What!" came the Chancellor's voice through the door.

"General Ashford is here, my lord," said the soldier.

"About damn time!" said the Chancellor. "Get in here, Ashford!"

The soldier stepped aside, and Garrick walked into the office. He stopped in front of the desk and stood at attention. He didn't see anyone else in the room, which was a bad sign.

Whoever the Chancellor had been screaming at was gone. Garrick glanced at the side door.

Silas had a crumpled piece of paper in his hand, which he threw at Garrick. It bounced off Garrick's chest and fell to the floor. Garrick didn't flinch. He waited for the Chancellor's permission to move.

"Well? Pick it up!" said Silas.

Garrick picked up the paper and read it. It was a report from a scribe. The scribe had gone into Silas's archive to leave a document and found someone already there. He never saw the person's face before he was knocked out.

*So, two people at least. One to steal and one to knock out the scribe.*

Garrick read the rest of the report. The thieves had tried to cover up the break-in by arranging several books as if they had fallen on the scribe. It did not look like anything had been taken.

"Books falling on him, as if anyone would believe that," said Silas with a sneer. "What simpletons!"

"My lord, what would you have of me?" asked Garrick.

"Find this thief! Find them and kill them!" said Silas. "They must not be allowed to ruin all that I have worked for!"

"My lord," said Garrick, and turned to leave.

"Find this... this... Ruiner! No, don't kill them. I want them tortured until they can't be healed anymore!" said Silas.

Garrick stopped at the door. He had walked out of a chamber once before, when the knife came out. He turned back.

"The Ruiner," said Silas again, as if tasting the word. "Yes. It was the Ruiner who broke the quarantine on the Plague Mage's farm. The Ruiner who is hiding Twelve from me. The Ruiner who stole Thirteen's body."

Garrick said nothing. He knew where Thirteen's body had gone.

Silas was breathing hard now. "And the kitchens! Months of thefts! Cheese! Candles! An entire ham!" He slammed his fist on the desk. "Months, Ashford. He has been robbing me for months."

Garrick had seen the kitchen boys eat ham.

"Yes, my lord," said Garrick, and left before the Chancellor could think of anything else.

Garrick went straight to the archives. He opened the bookcase with the switch beside the minotaur statue and stopped just inside the door. A small spot of blood marked the floor where the scribe had fallen, next to the books from the staged accident.

He looked around. The organization of the archives was haphazard at best. Garrick started at the wall with the door and worked his way counterclockwise around the room, checking each shelf for signs of tampering. On some of the shelves with older documents, the dust had been disturbed recently. A box of fresh paper sat neatly stacked, except for the top few sheets. And on the shelves that held more recent records, some of the papers seemed out of order.

The only shelves that seemed untouched were the ones on the wall to the left of the door. Garrick wondered if that meant anything. Were those documents of no interest to the thieves? Had they been interrupted before they could get to them?

*They took the old records, the ones no one checks, and copied the new ones. These weren't thieves. They were looking for something.*

As he stood there thinking about what the clues meant, he noticed a reddish smudge on the floor. He knelt down for a closer look. It looked like red powder. It was too soon for the blood to have dried into powder. He touched a little of it to his tongue. It tasted like dirt. Red dirt? No. Red clay, like he had seen on the boots of some of the guards. The guards who patrolled the dungeon levels.

Garrick stepped to the archive door, opened it, leaned out, and said, "Bring the scribe."

"Sir!" said one of the soldiers and left.

Garrick went back to examining the archive. A few moments later, the soldier knocked. Garrick opened the door and let the scribe in.

"Sit," said Garrick, pointing to a stool.

The scribe sat, looking around nervously.

"You said you didn't see who it was?" asked Garrick.

"Yes, sir. I mean, no, sir. I didn't see their face," said the scribe, wringing his hands.

"But you saw them?" asked Garrick.

"Yes, sir. Their back was to me when I came in," said the scribe.

"What were they wearing?" asked Garrick.

"Wearing? I don't know," said the scribe.

"So they were naked?" asked Garrick.

"No, they had on clothes. I just don't remember what they looked like, sir," said the scribe.

"Were they wearing armor? Were they dressed like people from the village? You must have noticed something about their clothes," said Garrick.

"He was... average, sir. Very average," said the scribe.

*He. At least that's something.* Garrick sighed, then dismissed the scribe.

Garrick headed down into the dungeon. At the guard station, he had the guards line up. They formed three lines of four and stood at attention. Garrick walked between the lines, looking at their boots. Each time he passed a guard whose boots had no red clay, he dismissed him.

Finally, four guards were left. All of them had patches of red clay on their boots.

"What are your assignments?" asked Garrick.

"Sir! We are assigned to the bottom cells of the dungeon, sir!" said one of the guards.

"Show me your logs for the day before yesterday," said Garrick.

"Sir!" said the guard, then immediately left the room. A few minutes later, he returned, huffing, with a ledger in hand. "Sir, this is our log."

Garrick took the ledger, flipped back to the day before yesterday, and started reading. The entries were repetitive until he reached one.

"A prisoner said he heard someone outside his cell?" asked Garrick.

"Sir! Yes, sir! But that was crazy Bran. He's always hearing things that aren't there," said the guard.

Except this time, he had probably heard something that was there.

Garrick closed the ledger. "Bring me Bran."
