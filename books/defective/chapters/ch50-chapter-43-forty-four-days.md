# Chapter 43: Forty-four Days

I turned to Prue. "You said a lot of things about me for the distraction. Everything you said was true, except one thing."

"Guess you have something to look forward to," said Prue with a smirk.

I looked at Mara. "What do I tell Plan B?"

"Tell them an assassin came and she's in the river. Tell them Prue and I were there. The rest is mine," said Mara. "That's your favor. Keep my secret. Evan, about Stroya..."

"I don't want to know. I mean that in the nicest possible way," I said.

Mara stared at me for a second, then smiled. It wasn't the Guild-desk one.

"Silas doesn't know she failed yet. You have a night or two," said Mara.

Then I kissed Prue good night. They left for their houses. Johan and I headed toward the chapel so I could grab an extra bedroll and blanket.

I couldn't take Johan into the chapel, because that would upset Amaranth, so we camped by the river near the boulder where Nara had carved the message.

We created a small fire. I offered to take first watch. I would wake him in three hours. Then we would break camp and head to the café for breakfast.

Johan took my extra bedroll and was able to get to sleep after several minutes.

When my watch was up, I woke Johan, then went to bed.

At dawn, I woke up. The sun had just started to light the sky. Johan was staring at the message that Nara carved.

"I wish this had been here sooner," said Johan.

I didn't have anything to say to that. *I wish it had been here sooner too.*

"How was it carved?" asked Johan.

"Nara used her wind magic and sand, kind of like a sandblaster," I said.

He looked over at me. "May I try something?"

"Sure," I said.

"Stone is stubborn," said Johan. "We'll need something to help us."

"Like sand," I said. He nodded.

I watched him create a tendril of water with his magic. Then he had it mix in some sand. The tendril began to spin like a drill bit. Johan directed the spinning tendril to a stone the size of a basketball. Within five minutes, it had drilled a hole through the stone. The edges were clean, and the hole was perfectly round.

*Well, that works.*

"Before we go to breakfast, can you give me a few minutes? I want to talk to Tired Guy."

"Yes."

*Tired Guy?* I asked.

*Hello, Evan,* a tired voice said.

*You sound extra tired today. Is there anything wrong?* I asked.

*The other gods all want the reset. I could not hold out any longer, but I did make them agree to a condition.*

*What condition?* I asked.

*That if the chamber still stands at the first snow, I will join the reset.*

*You're giving up on us?* I asked. It came out angrier than I meant.

*No. Without the condition, they would have begun already. Now they will wait for the snow.*

I opened my mouth to argue, then closed it. *The reset kills gods too.*

*Yes.*

*So if we fail, you...*

*Yes.*

*Why would you agree to that?*

A long pause. *Because I believe you can do this, Evan.*

I didn't know what to say to that. Nobody had ever bet anything on me before. Let alone everything. *No pressure.*

*Thank you,* I said.

*Be well, Evan, and good luck.*

I walked back over to Johan. "Let's go to the café."

We walked in silence.

When we got to the café, only Vagid was there. "Morning."

I nodded, then sat down. "Order whatever you want," I told Johan.

Nara arrived next, then Orrin. They ordered food. Vagid was already halfway through his breakfast. Johan's food came, then minutes later Nara and Orrin's.

"Didn't you get anything?" asked Nara.

"No. I'm not really hungry right now," I said.

After a few minutes, they had all finished. "Can we talk?" I asked.

Vagid nodded and led the way to the river landing.

"What is going on with you today, Evan?" asked Vagid.

"I talked to Tired Guy this morning."

"And?" said Orrin. "Bad news?"

"I don't know. He said he agreed to the reset," I said, "but only if the chamber was still standing at the first snow."

Nara stared at me. "Why would he do that?"

"What the heck?" said Orrin.

Johan just watched me.

Vagid thought for a moment. "He's betting on us, then?"

"Yeah," I said.

"And that worries you?" asked Vagid.

"It's a lot of pressure," I said.

"It's another job," said Vagid. "Like any other. We just need to plan it out and do the work. Just like before."

I looked up. Everyone was watching me.

"Yeah," I said. "And that's not the only news."

"What else?" asked Vagid.

"We got a tip from the general," I said. "Snow soon."

"Meaning?" asked Nara.

"I think he means the first snow," I said.

"The first snow where?" asked Nara.

"Since the ritual is at Thornspire, I think it means there," said Vagid.

"That makes sense," said Orrin.

"When does the first snow come up there?" I asked.

Vagid and Nara looked at each other. "Usually about a month and a half after harvest," said Nara.

*Not three months. Six weeks.*

"Thirteen embarrassed him. He won't wait," said Johan.

"Oh, and an assassin came for Johan. She found us at the curry house. Prue made a scene, the assassin got distracted, and now she's in the river."

Vagid's expression changed to worry. "Mara was there? Is she hurt?" He said it too quickly, then his ears flamed red.

"How did Prue distract the assassin?" asked Orrin.

"She said something about me swimming naked in the river, and some other stuff," I said.

"How did you take out an assassin?" asked Nara.

"Yeah, the one in the archives was too strong for you, but this one wasn't?" asked Orrin.

"Prue and Mara were there. That's all I can say. It's not my story to tell," I said.

Orrin started laughing. "She said you were found *naked*?"

"Yeah. Kind of loud too," I said.

I saw Nara watch me for a moment longer. Then she turned to Vagid. "We need to get our families out of Millbrook."

Vagid was still thinking about Mara being in trouble, so it took him a few seconds to catch up.

"But where would be safe?" asked Orrin.

Vagid was quiet for a moment. "Garnerby."

"Garnerby?" said Orrin.

"The whole town is rebuilding. Half the people there are strangers," said Vagid. "Where everyone is a stranger, no one is looking."

"Halden might be able to help with that," I said. "I'll ask him."

"That reminds me, where did you stay last night?" asked Nara. "You didn't go back to the chapel, did you?"

"No, we camped by the boulder with the message carved on it," I said. "Johan worked something out this morning."

"Really," said Nara. "What?"

"I'm going to have him show you, but he will be using his magic to do it," I said. "I just want you to be aware."

I motioned for Johan to come closer to the river. "Can you do the same thing as this morning? Maybe on a smaller stone though."

"I'll need sand," said Johan.

"There is a small sandbar near the other shore. That should work," I said.

Johan created the water tendril again, pulled in the sand, and recreated the drill. Then he proceeded to drill a hole in a softball-sized rock. Since it was a smaller stone, it took even less time. In three minutes, the hole was all the way through. Nara had taken a step back without seeming to notice.

"Wow," said Orrin. "It's just like an auger."

"A what?" I asked.

"It's a tool that carpenters use to create holes in timbers," said Orrin.

We all looked at Orrin like he had sprouted a second head.

"What? I know stuff," said Orrin.

"Without the sand, it would take hours or more to create a hole like that," said Johan. "Evan told me how you carved the words in the boulder by the river. It was very impressive."

"It was something that Evan and I came up with," said Nara.

"So how much sand do we need for the chamber?" asked Vagid.

"Well, we need a lot for the start, but then we would be making our own," said Johan.

"How much is 'a lot'?" asked Nara. "One bucket? Ten buckets?"

"The more the better," said Johan. "But we can probably figure that out with some experiments."

"Also, how far is the river from the entrance?" asked Vagid.

"It's around seven hundred paces," said Johan. Then, to me, "About a third of a mile."

"How will you get the water to the entrance and into the chamber?" I asked.

"If the river is that close, then there should be lots of groundwater," said Johan. "That should be enough."

"We have the timeline and the water. Now we just need to find enough sand," said Vagid. "The mine job we had should be very near there. They would have access to lots of sand."

"Oh! Good idea," I said.

"If we have time, we can move all the sand we want to near the entrance," said Vagid.

"Johan, can you give us any information on the chamber and the path from the entrance?" I asked. "Is the path from the entrance to the chamber straight, or curved? Does it have any tight turns?"

"It curves around. There are no sharp turns," said Johan.

"Okay, so how do we get the sand somewhere we can use it?" asked Vagid.

"What's the weather like around Thornspire?" I asked. "Since it's starting to turn into winter, is it more windy and rainy?"

"Yes. This time of year is very windy, and often there are storms," said Johan.

I looked at Nara. "Would it be possible to mix your magic with the existing winds to carry the sand into the entrance? As long as the sand gets scattered along the sides, people will probably ignore it."

Then I looked at Johan. "And when it rains, could you do the same thing with the rain?"

Nara thought for a minute, then said, "It's possible, but that would mean doing that for several days or even weeks."

"It is as Nara says. It will be difficult, but possible," said Johan.

"I think that Evan and I still need to do some work in Millbrook," said Vagid. "Nara, you should take Orrin and Johan and head to Thornspire. Get in touch with the mine foreman about the sand, then use the weather to hide moving it in. Orrin can be your lookout. Tell your families about Garnerby today. And remind them to keep it quiet."

"Forty-four days," said Vagid. Nobody said anything after that.

Nara looked across the landing at Johan. He met her eyes, which was more than he'd managed since the keep.

"Then we'd better learn to work together," she said.

Johan nodded once.

The wind off the river had a bite to it that hadn't been there yesterday. I could see my breath. *Forty-four days.*
