# Chapter 25: Status: Defective

The narrow stairs kept spiraling up. After a few minutes of climbing, Orrin held up his fist, and we all stopped. He pointed to a stone beside him, then at me. We all shuffled up a few steps so I could see it. It was another message from Rachel: "RL" and an arrow pointing up the stairs. I passed it along, and Orrin started climbing again.

After several more minutes, my legs were getting tired. I missed a step and started to topple backward, then jerked to a stop. Vagid had grabbed my pack, keeping me from taking us both down the stairs.

We sat on the stairs for a minute to rest, then kept climbing. After what seemed like an eternity, Orrin raised his fist again, and we all stopped.

"Door," said Orrin quietly.

Vagid held up his lantern, and we could all see another iron ring. Orrin leaned in to listen for anyone on the other side. Then he straightened and signaled Nara.

"Search," said Nara softly. After a few seconds, she added, "Clear."

Orrin grabbed the iron ring and pulled. With a soft click, the door swung open into a room.

The room was square, no more than ten feet across, and at least thirteen feet high. Shelves ran from floor to ceiling, all crammed with paper. The room smelled of old paper and lamp oil. The only bare spot was a door on the wall to the left of the opening we'd come through.

When Vagid came through, he pulled a scroll from the shelves and wedged it in the opening so the door couldn't close. *Now we have a way back out.*

Vagid pointed at Orrin, then at the door. Orrin spent a second examining it. The hinges were on the left as he faced it, so if it opened, it would hide the stair passage, as long as it didn't swing all the way back. Orrin took up a position on that side, listening and waiting.

"Anything with his seal. Thornspire. Units. Numbers used like names, like Twelve or Thirteen. And anything in squiggles, bring it to me," I said quietly.

Vagid, Nara, and I each took a wall to search. Nara took the wall with the opening to the narrow stairs, Vagid took the wall opposite the door, and I took the wall to the right of the door.

We spent almost fifteen minutes going through the documents and found dozens that met our search criteria. I skimmed each one and tried to keep only what we needed. At one point, Nara pulled a bundle, read a few lines, and went very still. She slid it into my pack without a word. We took anything old, because, as Vagid said under his breath, "No one's touched these in years." For the newer documents, I copied as much as I could onto some blank pages I found.

Suddenly, the door Orrin was watching started to swing into the room. There was nowhere to hide, so I kept my back to the door and my head down. Nara and Vagid knelt down and turned away from the door.

"Wha—" a scribe started to say, just as Orrin hit him with his sword pommel. The scribe collapsed and didn't move.

Orrin eased the door shut. Once I heard it click, I turned around. The scribe on the floor was the clerk from the inn, the one who had been worried about finishing his documents in time. *I guess his deadline isn't a problem anymore.* Vagid signaled for us to wrap everything up and leave.

Orrin shifted the unconscious scribe closer to the door, then arranged a few books around him as if they had fallen on him when he came in. Vagid and Nara quickly put back the documents they had pulled, and I returned the unused blank pages to where I'd found them. Nara knelt by the scribe to make sure he was breathing. We grabbed our packs, and Vagid pulled the scroll from the stair door and slid it back onto its shelf. We filed through, and Vagid closed the door behind us.

We crept back down the narrow staircase. We were all on edge, and nobody wanted to slip and tumble down several flights. At the bottom, we squeezed out through the oubliette, and the door clicked shut behind us. We made our way back down to the storeroom, crawled through the hole, and pulled the crates back over it on the hides.

Once we were back at camp, Orrin started a fire, Vagid began cooking dinner, and Nara and I read through the documents.

We started with the report about Rachel. Nara read it aloud.

> *Field Report, Retrieval of Subject Four (Plague)*
>
> *Subject tracked to an abandoned farmstead south of Barley Ford. Detachment of twenty-four, eighteen with crossbows. Per Subject's records, effective range of her ability is thirty paces. Detachment held at one hundred.*
>
> *Subject was standing on the porch. She called out a warning that we were too close. The commander judged the distance sufficient.*
>
> *Subject was struck by several bolts and fell. Within moments, the men in the front rank began coughing. Within the hour, nineteen were dead. The rear rank withdrew to the ridge. Two more died before morning.*
>
> *At dawn, crops and livestock as far as the ridge had failed. Subject was not observed to move.*
>
> *Documents: not recovered. Site considered unsafe. Recommend quarantine. Recommend Subject's records be amended: effective range underestimated.*

She told them they were too close.

None of us knew what to say about that. We just sat there for a moment.

Then I picked up my copy of the record of my summoning and read it aloud.

> *Summoning Record, Subject Thirteen*
>
> *Cost: four hundred units. Preparation: nine months.*
>
> *Result: no ability detected.*
>
> *Status: defective, disposed of.*

My hands dropped to my lap. There it was again: defective. *Am I really defective?* I sat there without moving, staring into the fire. Then I felt a hand on my arm. Nara was kneeling beside me. She smiled, and it wasn't until then that I realized I was crying. Not big sobs, just tears rolling down my cheeks.

"No," I said. "I'm not."

Nara smiled again and nodded.

There was one more bundle we hadn't read yet, the notes on failed experiments. It could wait. Two records were enough for one night.

Vagid handed out bowls of stew, and we ate without saying much. Afterward, I took the first watch. I sat near the mouth of the cave, where I could see the castle on the hill above us, its walls pale in the moonlight.

*Tired Guy?* I thought.

*Yes, Evan,* said Tired Guy.

*We made it into the archive,* I said.

*What did you find out?* asked Tired Guy.

*We found the assassins' report from when they killed Rachel. She died at the farm, but she tried to warn them. She tried to save them,* I said.

*A healer **to the end. That's fitting,* said Tired Guy.

*She loved helping and healing. I think she found peace in the end,* said Tired Guy. *Thank you for sharing this, Evan. It helps.*

*Good night,* I said.

I sat for a while, thinking about everything we had learned. I looked up at the castle walls above me. Somewhere in that castle, someone had decided I was defective. They were wrong.
