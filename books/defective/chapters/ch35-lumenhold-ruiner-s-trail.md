# Lumenhold — Ruiner's Trail

Garrick Ashford stood looking out the window of his office. The sight of soldiers training always calmed his nerves. The Chancellor had recently requested more frequent updates on the search for the Ruiner. Garrick was awaiting a report on just that subject. The report was due. He waited for the knock and watched the soldiers train. After a quarter of an hour, the soldiers had finished their training and were heading to dinner. He sighed, then buckled on his sword and fastened his cloak. He left his office and headed to Lumenhold. If the report was late, that meant something had gone wrong.

When Garrick arrived at Lumenhold, he immediately went to the archive. When the door to the archive opened, it was obvious why the report was late. The man who was supposed to deliver the report was lying dead on the archive floor.

Garrick had sealed the archive to set the trap, and no one had been in since. He believed that the Ruiner would attempt to get more documents from the archive, so he had arranged for an assassin to find out how the Ruiner had gotten into the archive. Garrick had specifically ordered the man to take them alive.

Garrick knelt to look at the dead man. He was dressed all in black, which was normal for his profession. There was a dagger with a small bit of blood on it. It looked like it belonged to the dead man. The dead man's clothes looked like they had been roughly searched. The rest of his weapons lay in a neat pile beside him. Whoever had done this was careful, even in a hurry. The same kind of people who had tucked a book under a scribe's head. What had the Ruiner been looking for? Then Garrick noticed a slight sheen on the dagger. One of them had been cut by a poisoned knife. The Ruiner had been searching for an antidote. A poisoned blade was not how you took anyone alive. This could only mean that the Chancellor had overridden Garrick's orders. Garrick filed that away, next to the knife.

The dead man had an arrow stabbed into his foot near a small smeared puddle of blood. An archer, then. Or someone very desperate. The blood smear led toward a set of shelves. Another hidden door, and one Garrick suspected not even the Chancellor knew about. Garrick looked at the main wound on the dead man. It was a single thrust, upward and twisted. Very efficient and experienced.

Done with the body, Garrick began his examination of the scene with the wall where the door was located. He went shelf by shelf. This time he found where dust had been disturbed, indicating that documents were removed or moved since the room was sealed. Garrick would need to question the scribes on what documents were located on the shelves. One of them should know what those documents contained.

Garrick turned his attention back to the blood smear that led to a set of shelves. He tried every knob and carving on the shelves, looking for the trigger. It took several minutes to determine what the trigger was, but eventually he heard a click, and the door swung open. The blood smear continued into the dark opening.

Garrick went back to the archive door, opened it, and requested three soldiers be sent to him. When the soldiers arrived, Garrick met them in the tower base.

"What you will be seeing is a state secret. Speak of it to anyone and you will answer to me. Am I clear?" said Garrick.

"Sir!" answered all the soldiers.

Garrick led them into the archive. The soldiers lit torches and then followed Garrick into the hidden stairway. It took them over an hour to reach the end of the stairway. By the halfway point, the youngest soldier was breathing hard. Garrick was not, and made sure they all noticed. Garrick saw the iron ring and pulled it. The door opened with a soft click.

Garrick knelt and crawled through the opening, then stood up and waited for the soldiers to join him. This appeared to be a level of Lumenhold's dungeon. But there were no people here. It had been sealed off long ago and forgotten. Garrick studied the opening from the dungeon side. It looked like an oubliette door. Near the bottom were squiggles carved into the stones. Though Garrick could not read the language of the Otherborn, he could still recognize it. One of them had carved something here, long ago. The marks were low, near the floor, where a man would carve them sitting down. Someone had been kept here. No one from this world could read what the message meant. Whoever had been here had either found the switch by luck, or could read it. Garrick did not like where the second thought led, so he set it aside. For now.

Garrick started down the hallway, and the soldiers followed. They eventually reached stone steps leading up and ascended only to find the top of the stairs walled over. Garrick put his ear near the wall and heard the guards yelling at Bran for making a fuss. Something about a goat. This section of the dungeon had been walled off. That left the other end of the hallway to explore. Garrick led the soldiers back down the hallway past the oubliette door. A few minutes later they found the stairs down to the next level.&#32;

They descended to the next level and followed the hallway until it reached an end. No more stairs. Garrick had the soldiers look for any signs that someone had been through here recently. There were footprints in the dust on the floor, and they all led to a door near the end of the hallway. Garrick opened the door and found a wall of crates stacked up.

"Move those crates out of the room," said Garrick. The soldiers immediately began taking the crates out and stacking them in the hallway. Behind the crates was a hole in the wall. Someone had gathered crates from all over the dungeon to hide it. Garrick took a torch and led the way through the hole and into a natural cave. They were at the end of a branch. He led the group out of the cul-de-sac away from the storeroom. They reached an intersection. Garrick had the soldiers search the area for any signs of recent activity.

Next to the firepit that showed recent use, they found footprints leading down both branches. The newest set sank much deeper than the rest. One of the group was being carried, so those were the tracks of the ones who killed the assassin. Beside the firepit lay the rind of a cheese. Garrick thought of the Chancellor slamming his fist on the desk over cheese, and for the first time that day, he almost smiled.

Garrick found a small pool of what looked like green goblin blood, but goblins didn't usually inhabit caves. Caves were usually where you'd find kobolds, and their blood was a brownish red.

"Sir!" called one of the soldiers. Garrick walked over to where the soldier was kneeling. The soldier pointed to some bloodied bandages. "One of the intruders was injured."

"Yes," said Garrick. "How old would you say those bandages are?"

"No more than a day, maybe two days," said the soldier.

Garrick looked back at the green stain. Goblin blood, in a kobold cave. Whoever had been here had come from a goblin job, or was going to one.

And the Guild kept a record of every goblin job.
