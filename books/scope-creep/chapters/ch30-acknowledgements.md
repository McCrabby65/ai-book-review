## Acknowledgements

Every book is a project, and every project runs over. This one was estimated at a few months. I did not have Mike's skill. Nobody told me how long it would really take, and if they had, I wouldn't have listened. That, it turns out, is the whole book.

So, a few thank-yous, ranked top to bottom, no ties. (I'm kidding. There are ties everywhere. Please don't tell Mike.)

**To Kris, Ethan, Trevor, Seth and Lily**, who heard "I just need to finish this one chapter" more times than any stakeholder should ever have to, and who never once asked for a status update at four o'clock on a Friday. You are the stand-up that takes fifteen minutes. Thank you.

**To my beta readers, who found the bugs before production did, and who filed their reports in black ink, on the right side, after knocking three times. Every typo that survived is mine. Please log it.

---

And then there are the people I've never met, but who are in every chapter anyway.

To every developer who has been handed a deadline *before* anyone asked for an estimate. To everyone who has said "that'll take three weeks" and been told "great, so Thursday." You were right. You were always right. It's just that nobody was listening.

To the Durgans. The people in the basement who keep the old system running, who know which wall holds up the building, and who have never once been invited to the launch party. Somebody should give you a medal. Somebody should at least move their papers over and make room for your sandwich.

To everyone who has ever written documentation. Three hundred and twelve pages, and an index, and nobody reads it, until the day everyone does. You are the reason the new hire's first day didn't feel like stepping off a cliff.

To everyone who has stood up in a meeting and said, "Not on a Friday." You were heroes. You were also correct.

To the blameless retrospective, and the five whys, and the person who actually wrote the notes.

And, with some reluctance, to every Chief Visionary Officer, Chief Ink Officer, and Chief Quill Officer out there. Without you, there would be no satire. Somewhere, right now, one of you is leaning across a bar and putting a hand flat on the counter. Thank you for your service. Please don't synergize with my guild.

---

Finally, to you, for reading all the way to the acknowledgements. Nobody reads the acknowledgements. You are clearly the kind of person who reads the whole guide.

I asked the skill one last question before I sent this book out into the world.

**Estimated time until the sequel: Unknown.**

Which, as Mike would tell you, is the most honest estimate there is.

*— Paul McSwain* *North Carolina**, 2026*
